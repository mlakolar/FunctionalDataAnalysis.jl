## Executive summary

The package has a substantial, independently referenced implementation and is
ready for focused consolidation. This audit does not support a wholesale
rewrite, a blanket naming migration, deletion of the reference fixtures, or
removal of the remaining specialized smoothers. The most valuable work before
the API hardens is to settle public result/accessor contracts, remove measured
allocation bottlenecks, and make the development test cycle cheaper and more
reproducible.

The main findings are:

- Covariance smoothing dominates PACE in the measured sparse workloads:
  5,000 subjects/grid 101 take 26.37 seconds at one thread and 3.94 seconds
  at eight. Thread gains vary sharply by route; they are not universal.
- Narrow external probes support complete-grid curve batching (8.51–16.99×),
  exact repeated-pair surface compression (19.60×, surface only), and hoisting
  sparse-FSVD grid invariants (91.3% fewer CE-kernel bytes at grid 201).
  Their synthetic parity is strong; production R-reference gates remain required.
- Settle reconstruction-generic ownership, prediction/result shapes, and
  FSVD/CCA accessors before field layouts become permanent. Three stale
  overview statements need correction despite complete symbol-level docstrings.
- Both audited revisions have one sign-sensitive analytic test failure; the
  current suite records 6,246 passes/one failure. Aqua and strict R goldens pass. Sign-aligned eigenfunctions agree at `2.82e-14`.
  The sparse-FSVD interpolation-rank restriction also needs a specific diagnosis;
  increasing grid noise cannot repair a rank-deficient interpolation design.

The recommendations preserve the package's statistical contracts. **Every
numerical follow-up must retain the existing aligned fdapace/refund comparisons
at relative error below `1e-8`, and former-smoother agreement at `1e-10`. The new face/fda routes must likewise
retain their documented aligned `1e-8` comparisons, without claiming native
quantities agree when the documented metric conversion differs.** Native
R quantities and package quantities deliberately differ in quadrature, noise
integration, FACE posterior variance, and derivative representation; converting
those departures into accidental defaults would be a regression. Float32 smoke
tests need their own justified tolerance and do not replace Float64 goldens.

Severity: **P1** = high-priority scaling/API-hardening issue; **P2** = material
maintenance, contract, or development-cost issue; **P3** = useful lower-priority
cleanup. Effort estimates include focused tests and docs, in developer days.
“Breaking” includes generic identity, result shape, struct layout, and accepted
input changes even when numerical estimates remain identical. No P0 finding was
established.

## 1. Redundancy, dead code, and migration leftovers

### R1. Share fold construction without changing the CV criterion

**P2 · 0.5–1 day · nonbreaking.** `src/mean.jl:186–195` and
`src/covariance.jl:163–171` duplicate the folds/nobs check, copied-RNG shuffle,
balanced `mod1` assignment, and leave-one-curve-out case. Both selection results
retain `(bandwidths, errors, assignments)`. Extract one `_curve_folds` helper
and retain exact assignments, RNG nonmutation, candidate order, and tie behavior.

The loss backends should remain separate. Mean CV (`mean.jl:196–205`) holds out
whole curves; covariance CV (`covariance.jl:174–179`) delegates grouped bivariate
CV to KernelSmoothing and restores within-cell scatter. Replacing the former
with ordinary one-dimensional observation CV changes the estimator. The
whole-curve choice is documented at `docs/src/fdapace.md:80–81`.

### R2. Consolidate interpolation and grid validation at the correct boundary

**P2 · 1–2 days · nonbreaking.** Three scalar bilinear kernels occur at
`src/pace.jl:132–139`, `src/covariance.jl:286–294`, and
`src/cross_covariance.jl:183–193`. A private two-axis core can serve all three;
public wrappers must preserve range checks and the current arithmetic order.
Reconstruction also repeats score/grid validation and mean/basis assembly at
`src/fpca.jl:534–544`, `src/derivatives.jl:256–269`, and
`src/multivariate_fpca.jl:351–363`. Keep ID and derivative-metadata handling in
the wrappers rather than introducing a large new model hierarchy.

**P3 · 0.5–1 day · nonbreaking.** `quadrature_weights` both validates and
allocates weights (`src/quadrature.jl:10–21`). Some callers discard those
weights (`smoothing.jl:104`, `fpca.jl:540`, `derivatives.jl:262`). Extract a
strict-grid validator, distinct from arbitrary evaluation-point validation
(`evaluation.jl:21–26`). Preserve positive user-supplied FPCBasis weights
(`fpcbasis.jl:29–45`). This is a small structural cleanup; no speedup is claimed
without a dedicated comparison.

### R3. Reuse spline fitting preparation, keeping distinct penalties distinct

**P2 · 1–2 days · nonbreaking.** `_gcv_selection`
(`src/smoothing.jl:138–171`) and `to_basis`
(`src/conversions.jl:91–110`) repeat basis designs, pivoted-QR/rank handling,

## 2. Performance, allocations, and inference

### P1. Sparse FSVD repeats subject-invariant grid work

**P1 · 1–2 days · nonbreaking if confined to internal work.**
`src/cross_structure.jl:77–100` allocates two full identities and copies the
same projected covariance for every subject, then adds the same marginal noise
(`:82–91`). Interpolation matrices/block products also scale with work-grid
length even though each subject has few observations.

A surgical external copy of the CE-score helper hoists only the two identities and regularized covariance. It uses supplied grid covariance/cross-covariance to isolate scoring, with eight observations per curve, five interleaved original/hoisted rounds, Julia/BLAS 1/1. It is **not an end-to-end FSVD speedup**.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 51 | 6.119 | 4.912 | 1.25× | 17.74 | 5.92 |
| 500 | 51 | 23.464 | 17.671 | 1.33× | 83.78 | 24.16 |
| 1,000 | 51 | 46.072 | 33.393 | 1.38× | 166.30 | 46.95 |
| 100 | 201 | 87.817 | 47.138 | 1.86× | 215.46 | 32.33 |
| 500 | 201 | 290.540 | 165.869 | 1.75× | 1,021.40 | 98.35 |
| 1,000 | 201 | 467.359 | 301.053 | 1.55× | 2,024.02 | 176.06 |

All six sizes gave **zero measured absolute/relative difference** in both left/right score matrices. At 1,000 subjects/grid 201, cumulative allocation falls from 2,024.02 to 176.06 MiB (91.3%); runtime improves 1.55×. The unchanged package was not replaced by this helper, so all R goldens must still be replayed in an implementation PR.

First hoist identities and the regularized covariance. A subsequent direct
two-neighbor interpolation implementation can remove identities entirely, but
is not covered by the experiment. Preserve the full stacked PSD projection and
**noise-before-interpolation** ordering. A selected-K-only or differently
regularized substitute would change the existing FSVD contract.

**P1 for diagnosis/docs · 0.5–1 day · no numerical change; exception details
are observable.** The failed uniform sparse case exposes a separate
interpolation restriction. Subject 310 has an `8×101` interpolation matrix
of numerical rank **7**, smallest singular value `1.70e-17`; **2/1,000**
subject designs lose row rank. Three observations in one linear-interpolation
interval have only two support columns. With a left-null vector of `H`,
`H*A*H'` remains singular for every grid covariance `A`, including positive
grid noise. Increasing noise can perturb Cholesky's floating-point verdict
but cannot restore rank. This diagnosis is independent of the self-paired
stress case's correlated-error issue.

The existing noise-before-interpolation ordering and rejection of singular
observation covariance are deliberate (`docs/src/fdapace.md:762`;
`src/cross_structure.jl:80–93`). The exception at `src/pace.jl:180` and advice
at `docs/src/cross_structure.md:56–62` suggest positive noise without this
qualification. Diagnose interpolation rank **on factorization failure**,
explain the grid/design restriction, and add a deterministic three-in-one-cell
test. Do not silently move noise after interpolation, add a ridge, or use a
pseudoinverse: those would change the estimator and its reference agreement.

### P2. Dense FPCA/FSVD retain grid-square work even with a small component cap

**P2 · 2–4 days for a bounded buffer/storage PR · potentially
breaking if stored covariance/spectrum contracts change.** Dense FPCA forms
the full sample covariance (`src/fpca.jl:376–377`); `_fpca_eigen` forms a
weighted matrix and computes its complete eigendecomposition before `maxK`
truncation (`:258–275`). It then reconstructs/stores another grid-square
projected covariance (`:387–388`). Dense FSVD fits both marginals and computes
a full weighted cross-surface SVD (`src/cross_structure.jl:140–142,161–164`).
The benchmark table quantifies the resulting long-grid baseline rather than
claiming that a partial solver is already a safe replacement.

Start with buffer reuse and avoiding redundant copies while retaining public
storage. Investigate an exact dual-Gram route only for the appropriate wide
data case; test all spectrum/negative-spectrum diagnostics, FVE normalization,
sign rules, and projected covariance, not just leading eigenvectors. An
approximate or selected-K eigensolver cannot automatically preserve the hard
`1e-8` requirement. FACE already provides a distinct low-rank estimator; it is
not a numerically interchangeable acceleration of dense FPCA.

### P3. Raw-pool preparation repeatedly scans all subject labels

**P2 · 1 day · nonbreaking.** `_covariance_pool` scans `subjects` with
`findall(==(i),subjects)` for every curve (`src/covariance.jl:143–152`);
`_cross_pool` does this for both nodes (`src/cross_covariance.jl:115–122`).
The pool-only benchmark isolates preparation from smoothing; see its 1,000-
and 5,000-subject measurements above. Use already-contiguous subject ranges
or one offsets pass, preserving pair iteration order, weights, binning, and
within-cell scatter. This removes repeated scans, not the statistically
required within-curve pair products. An index-only three-repetition probe confirms identical subject index lists:
at 1,000 subjects, repeated `findall` takes 0.742 ms/0.951 MiB versus
0.025 ms/0.111 MiB for precomputed ranges; at 5,000, 15.565 ms/19.059 MiB
versus 0.449 ms/0.557 MiB. Offset construction is excluded. This does not
measure an optimized full pool: existing complete-pool medians are 1.927 ms
and 21.032 ms, small beside 5.124/26.374-second covariance fits. It is a
bounded preparation improvement, not the dominant kernel bottleneck.

### P4. Curve smoothing and FACE have concrete workspace opportunities

**P1 for the measured complete-grid curve path; P2 for other work · 1–2 days
per bounded PR · internal changes nonbreaking; public `!` APIs additive.**
The #27 curve smoother correctly reuses one matrix for identical observation
times. It still obtains copied row data per subject, performs `L*y` per row,
stages through `Matrix{Union{Missing,T}}`, and copies into the final owned
container (`src/data_interface.jl:44–48`; `smoothing.jl:106–130`). Its cache
retains every distinct smoother matrix and explicitly stores Float64 matrices
(`:108`), including Float32 input. Use the measured dense/irregular baselines
to size the benefit of a complete-grid block multiplication path and bounded
cache/workspace policy. Preserve missingness, edge coverage, and numerical
agreement; BLAS batching may change reduction order.

An external complete-Float64, matching-grid block probe preserves IDs/metadata
and uses `values * L'`, one smoother matrix, and numeric output. Five
interleaved original/block rounds at 1/1 threads give:

| Subjects × grid | Original ms / MiB | Block ms / MiB | Speedup | Max absolute / relative difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 × 101 | 11.982 / 13.40 | 0.705 / 1.14 | 16.99× | `2.66e-15` / `1.54e-16` |

This supports a narrow production fast path, with an 88.6% byte reduction in

FACE is already grid-linear in its covariance factors
(`src/face.jl:1–9,181–183`); the 5,001/20,001-grid benchmarks exercise that

Missing fits allocate two complemented masks per iteration (`:156`), an

MFPCA also has weighted/scaled/centered score temporaries
(`src/mfpca.jl:95–118`). Its uncentered normalization is intentional R behavior;
“center everything” is not a valid allocation fix. Existing matrix products
already use LinearAlgebra. The BLAS-thread experiment above should guide a
documented configuration; do not assume more BLAS threads improve these sizes.

### P5. Concrete payload types are lost at selected wrapper boundaries

**P2 · 1–3 days for one wrapper at a time · struct parameter/layout changes can
be breaking.** Key sites are `BSplineBasis.backend::BSplineKit.BSplineBasis`

The runtime inference/JET inventory above distinguishes actual dispatch sites

(both marginal fits erased). The baseline FPCA instances have 20/2, both
concrete unions. These counts exclude erasure inside concrete containers such

### P6. Exact duplicate pair compression exists only in the benchmark path

**P1 for common-grid covariance · 1–2 days for fixed fits · nonbreaking if
coordinates, weights, provenance, and numerical behavior are retained.**
Production appends every off-diagonal subject pair
(`src/covariance.jl:143–152`) and only aggregates cells when optional binning
is enabled, within each subject (`:116–122,147`). The fixed surface consumes
that pool directly (`:209–220`). Common-grid inputs therefore send repeated
coordinates across subjects to the bivariate smoother.

`benchmark/sigma2/common.jl:16–25,95–105` already implements exact-coordinate
weighted sufficient-statistic compression. Its CV helper compresses training
and held-out pools separately (`:40–46`). This is exact grouping, not snapping
times to new knots; the equivalence is already documented at
`docs/src/fdapace.md:35–36`. Existing checks cover first/second weighted moments,
grouped CV losses, surfaces, and all four noise-estimator variants
(`benchmark/sigma2/check.jl:10–47`, tolerances `1e-12`/`1e-10`). Those checks
were inspected, not freshly rerun in this audit.

The external surface-only probe uses 20 subjects on 51 identical grid points,
Gaussian bandwidth `.2`, degree one: **51,000 raw off-diagonal pairs → 2,550
exact coordinate groups**. Over three interleaved repetitions at 1/1 threads,
the raw surface median is **2,096.766 ms / 5.89 MiB**; compressed is
**106.999 ms / 3.29 MiB**, a **19.60×** surface speedup. Compression preparation
separately takes **2.201 ms / 0.369 MiB**. Surface maximum absolute difference
is `1.27e-13`, relative Frobenius difference `1.05e-13`.
This excludes CV, noise integration, and the full covariance fit; it does not
claim a 19.60× production end-to-end improvement. Raw measurements and the
exact grouping recipe are in `pool_probe.csv`/`pool_probe.jl`.

A first production PR should cover fixed bandwidths and both weightings,
preserve original diagonal observations, and replay all downstream goldens.
Do not aggregate across subjects before holding a subject out for CV. Changes
in accumulation order or singular-window behavior still have to meet the hard
numerical requirement; an algebraic sufficient-statistic argument alone is
not a validation result.

### P7. New routes retain fixed-fit diagnostics and repeat CV/display work

**Current · P2 · 1–3 days per bounded preparation PR · nonbreaking if result
and diagnostic contracts remain unchanged.** Sparse FACE uses parameterized,
low-rank exact-spline covariance (`src/face_sparse.jl:4–16`), but stores dense
pair designs and one parameter-Gram matrix per subject (`:61–82,163–195`).
At default 10 basis functions, 56 covariance/noise parameters imply 125,440,000
bytes for 5,000 subjects' Float64 local Grams alone; this is a **derived storage
count**, not peak memory. The current-head measurements above give allocated
bytes. Group sizes copy each curve twice (`:171`) despite available offsets.
Default and fixed lambda fits both compute and retain full mean/covariance
criteria (`:117–124,194–195`), intentionally protected by fixed-case goldens.
Do not discard those diagnostics as an invisible performance change.

Basis FPCA's `LambdaCV` copies each held-in training matrix and repeats a
subject-invariant, candidate-specific penalized Gram factorization for every subject
(**current** `src/smoothed_fpca.jl:90–92,125–136`). Cache candidate metrics and
consider validated leave-out moment updates; preserve overall-mean centering
and L² subspace solves, because penalized harmonics are not generally mutually
L²-orthogonal. CCA CV instead centers within each training fold and repeats
means/moments across candidates (`:344–354`): those semantics must remain
separate. Fixed basis FPCA also materializes two display-grid-square covariance
matrices (`:218–220`), despite exact low-dimensional coefficients. Its measured
201-versus-2,001 display-grid allocation cost is reported above. A lazy
covariance could help, but public storage/materialization compatibility needs
an explicit decision.

## 3. Julia idiom and public interface

### J1. Naming is largely idiomatic; resolve generic ownership before renaming

**P2 · 0.5–1 day · generic identity changes are breaking.** The explicit export
list contains 87 names: 34 types and 53 functions; none of the function names
uses mixed case. The 29 underscore-containing function names are permitted by
the [Julia style guide](https://docs.julialang.org/en/v1/manual/style-guide/#Use-naming-conventions-consistent-with-Julia-base/).
There is no useful basis for a blanket underscore/acronym migration.
`nobs`, `fit`, and `predict` use StatsAPI, and `eigvals` extends LinearAlgebra
(`src/FunctionalDataAnalysis.jl:4`; `src/fpca.jl:429`). Internal mutators such
as `_evaluate_curve!` and `_fpca_orient!` use `!` correctly. No public
argument-mutating function lacking `!` was established; absence of exported
in-place variants is a design opportunity rather than a naming error.

FDA owns its own `reconstruct` (`fpca.jl:534`), while the installed StatsAPI
1.8.0 [already defines that generic](https://github.com/JuliaStats/StatsAPI.jl/blob/main/src/regressionmodel.jl).
MultivariateStats separately exports `reconstruct` and `evaluate` in its
[official module](https://github.com/JuliaStats/MultivariateStats.jl/blob/master/src/MultivariateStats.jl).
Choose canonical ownership and a suitable minimum StatsAPI version now; avoid
adding a heavy dependency simply to merge names. Document qualified `evaluate`
imports if its independent ownership remains intentional.

**P3 · 0.5–1 day for one additive buffer API · nonbreaking.** There are zero
exported `!` functions. A bounded `evaluate!(out, fd, points, evaluator)` could
reuse the existing `_evaluate_curve!` (`src/evaluation.jl:37–73`), while a
future `reconstruct!` should follow the generic-ownership decision and define
buffer shape/aliasing explicitly (`src/fpca.jl:534–544`). These are missing
repeated-call options, not evidence that every output allocation is avoidable.
No isolated public-buffer speedup was measured; prioritize the demonstrated
internal fast paths first.

### J2. Prediction shape and statistical accessors need explicit contracts

**P2 · 1–2 days · additive accessors nonbreaking; changing `predict` is breaking.**
Dense FPCA predicts a matrix; PACE/FACE predict `(scores,covariances)`
(`src/fpca.jl:483–507`). Multivariate prediction propagates this into a vector
of alternatives (`multivariate_fpca.jl:255–282`); MFPCA needs adapters to extract
scores (`mfpca.jl:180–181,193–203`). Settle whether score projection and
uncertainty-bearing prediction should have separate explicit accessors/results.
Retain current behavior through deprecation if changing the result contract.
Smoother `fit` methods return transformed functional data rather than a model
with `predict` (`src/smoothing.jl:192–195`); the interface table should say so
explicitly, alongside decomposition fits and `smooth_curves`.

There are no FDA methods for StatsAPI `fitted`, `residuals`, or `coef`.
This is not a mandatory RegressionModel-interface violation: the decomposition
types do not subtype that abstraction. The
[StatsBase model interface](https://juliastats.org/StatsBase.jl/latest/statmodels/)
provides useful meanings, not a requirement to invent meaningless coefficients.
Add methods only with precise definitions: fitted latent curves versus noisy
observations, residuals at observed times for irregular/masked data, and
coefficients only where their representation is unambiguous.

**P2 · 0.5–1 day · additive/nonbreaking.** FSVD currently exposes values,
functions, scores, and FVE as fields (`src/cross_structure.jl:41–53`) without
`nobs`, `ncomponents`, `svdvals`, `fve`, or left/right score/function accessors.
Its examples use fields (`docs/src/cross_structure.md:17–21`). Add a consistent
accessor layer now, documenting owned storage versus copies. Use singular-value
terminology rather than calling these eigenvalues.

### J3. Generic input support is real; specify scalar-type and validation edges

**P2 · 0.5–1 day · preserving existing behavior nonbreaking.** Constructors
accept one-based AbstractMatrix/AbstractVector inputs and intentionally make
owned copies (`src/data.jl:29–50,91–149,175–183`). Concrete internal storage is
not itself a failure to accept views or ranges. Float32 tests already cover
containers, bases, dense FPCA, supplied-input PACE, FACE, MFPCA, and FSVD;
additional gaps are smoother-cache precision, fully estimated PACE, and a few
view/range paths. Runtime probe outcomes are recorded above. Offset indexing is
explicitly rejected; supporting it would be new scope.

**P3 · 0.5 day · rejecting formerly accepted inputs is a small breaking change.**
Bool count validation is inconsistent: MFPCA/FSVD/FACE reject it explicitly,
whereas `_fpca_selection(::Integer)`, dense `maxK`, and `_derivative_order`
accept `true` as one (`src/fpca.jl:306–310,367–369`; `src/bases.jl:42–45`).
Choose one integer-validation rule and test it consistently.

Multiple dispatch is already used well for providers, evaluators, bandwidth
rules, preprocessing, and CE solvers. A small symbol switch for a public
algorithm is not automatically unidiomatic; even
[MultivariateStats.PCA](https://juliastats.org/MultivariateStats.jl/stable/pca/)
uses algorithm symbols. Typed settings may simplify validation, but no speed
claim follows from replacing keywords alone.

### J4. Current-main additions extend the accessor/settings work

**Current · P2 · 1 day · additive accessors nonbreaking; renames/type layouts
potentially breaking.** Current exports total 89 names: 36 types and the same
