Penalized fitting

PenalizedLeastSquares is the engine behind penalized regression. For a Gaussian response with the identity link it minimizes

\[\|W^{1/2}(y - X\beta)\|^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{M} \lambda_j \beta^\top S_j \beta\]

for a design X, known positive observation weights W, and symmetric positive semidefinite penalties S_j. Each penalty acts on a block of columns and may be rank deficient. Several penalties may act on the same block, as for the two marginal roughness penalties of a tensor-product coefficient. Columns in no penalty are unpenalized. The smoothing parameters λ_j are fixed, or chosen by REML (the default) or GCV. Binomial, Poisson and non-identity Gaussian responses are fitted by penalized iteratively reweighted least squares with Laplace-approximate REML, GCV or UBRE; see Exponential-family responses.

using FunctionalDataAnalysis
import StatsAPI

t = collect(range(0.0, 1.0; length=60))
z = cos.(7 .* t)
y = 0.5 .* z .+ sin.(2pi .* t) .+ 0.2 .* sin.(37 .* t .^ 2)
basis = BSplineBasis(4, (0.0, 1.0), 12)
X = hcat(z, basis_matrix(basis, t))
# One roughness penalty on the spline block; column 1 is unpenalized.
model = StatsAPI.fit(PenalizedLeastSquares, X, y;
    penalties=[penalty(basis, 2) => 2:13])
(model.lambda, model.lambda_status, model.edf_total, model.sigma2)
([4.802898449856427e-5], [:estimated], 9.8413465110864, 0.012965618296685393)
FunctionalDataAnalysis.PenalizedLeastSquares — Type
PenalizedLeastSquares

Result of StatsAPI.fit(PenalizedLeastSquares, X, y; penalties, ...), which minimizes

‖W^{1/2}(y - Xβ)‖² + Σⱼ λⱼ βᵀSⱼβ

over β for known positive observation weights W or, for an exponential-family response, the penalized deviance D(β) + Σⱼ λⱼ βᵀSⱼβ by penalized iteratively reweighted least squares (PIRLS). Each penalty Sⱼ is symmetric positive semidefinite, may be rank deficient, and acts on a block of columns. Several penalties may share columns (tensor products); columns in no penalty are unpenalized.

fit(PenalizedLeastSquares, X, y; penalties, weights=nothing,
    family=:gaussian, link=nothing, criterion=:REML, lambda=nothing,
    lower=nothing, upper=nothing, rank_tol=eps(T)^(3/4), tol=1e-8,
    maxiter=200, irls_tol=1e-12, irls_maxiter=200)

penalties is a vector whose entries are S => columns pairs (a square matrix and the distinct indices of the columns of X it acts on), or full size(X, 2)-square matrices. weights defaults to ones (for the binomial family they are the numbers of trials, y the proportions of successes).

family is :gaussian (default), :binomial or :poisson; link=nothing selects the canonical link (:identity, :logit, :log). Other links are :log and :inverse (Gaussian), :probit and :cloglog (binomial), :identity and :sqrt (Poisson), as R's family objects. Binomial responses must lie in [0, 1] with integer trials and successes, Poisson responses must be nonnegative integers, and Gaussian responses must be positive for the log link and nonzero for the inverse link (R's starting values); anything else throws.

criterion=:REML (the default, as in refund's pfr/pffr), :GCV or :UBRE selects mgcv 1.9-3's method="REML" or method="GCV.Cp", which is GCV when the scale is unknown (Gaussian family) and UBRE when it is known (one, for the binomial and Poisson families); the other combinations throw:

  • REML, Gaussian identity link: V = Dₚ/(2φ) + (n-Mₚ)/2 log(2πφ) + log|XᵀWX + S_λ|/2 - log|S_λ|₊/2 - Σ log wᵢ/2, with Dₚ the penalized weighted residual sum of squares, Mₚ the dimension of the null space of S_λ = Σ λⱼSⱼ and φ profiled out at Dₚ/(n-Mₚ).
  • Laplace-approximate REML otherwise: V = Dₚ/(2φ) - ℓₛ + log|H|/2 - log|S_λ|₊/2 - Mₚ/2 log(2πφ) with Dₚ the penalized deviance, ℓₛ the saturated log-likelihood, H = XᵀWₙX + S_λ the Hessian of Dₚ/2 (Newton weights Wₙ, mgcv's alpha-weighted ones for non-canonical links), φ = 1 for binomial and Poisson and profiled at Dₚ/(n-Mₚ) for the Gaussian family (mgcv optimizes log φ jointly; the minimum is the same).
  • GCV: V = n D/(n - τ)²; UBRE: V = D/n + 2τ/n - 1; with D the (weighted) deviance and τ = tr(A) the trace of the influence matrix, computed with Fisher weights W_F as mgcv does.

PIRLS (mgcv gam.fit3) takes Newton steps on the penalized deviance, with Fisher scoring when the Newton Hessian is not positive definite and step halving while the penalized deviance does not decrease or the fit leaves the family's domain. The first fit starts from R's family mustart with one Fisher step, later fits from the previous coefficients. It stops once the Newton decrement is at most irls_tol times 1 + Dₚ (after that final step) and throws after irls_maxiter iterations. A fit with fitted probabilities within 10eps of 0 or 1, or Poisson means within 10eps of 0 (where mgcv and glm warn), is rejected: the deviance is still decreasing along a diverging direction, as when binomial classes are separated. During selection such smoothing parameters are inadmissible; otherwise the fit throws. irls_tol=1e-12 is tighter than mgcv's epsilon=1e-7, a package choice so that coefficients are converged in working precision.

Smoothing parameters are fixed by passing lambda (finite nonnegative values), or optimized on log λ within lower and upper (defaults 0 and Inf; a parameter with equal bounds is fixed). Newton's method uses exact first and second derivatives (for PIRLS fits including the derivatives of the weights, after Wood 2011), eigenvalue-floored Hessians, steps of at most 5 on the log scale and up to 30 step halvings (mgcv newton defaults). It starts from mgcv's initial.sp heuristic applied to the weighted design (initial.spg: Fisher weights at the starting means for PIRLS fits), clamped to the bounds. It stops (settings.convergence) at :gradient when every free gradient entry is at most tol times |V| (GCV) or 1 + |V| (REML, UBRE) and no parameter without curvature can still decrease V, at :no_decrease when no halved step decreases V, or at :maxiter. tol=1e-8 is tighter than mgcv's conv.tol=1e-6, so that the optimum is at least as good as mgcv's; maxiter=200 and rank_tol are package choices. Determinants and solves use Wood's (2011, appendix B) similarity transform for every group of overlapping penalties followed by diagonally scaled Cholesky factors, which keeps them accurate when smoothing parameters differ by many orders of magnitude.

A direction the data do not identify drives its λⱼ towards infinity (or, for GCV, zero). That parameter then moves in steps of 5 on the log scale while V still decreases in working precision, up to e^60 times its initial value, and lambda_status records :effectively_infinite (or :effectively_zero). Other statuses are :estimated, :fixed, :lower_bound and :upper_bound. rank_tol is the relative eigenvalue threshold that determines the null space of the penalties. Responses fitted exactly (penalized residual sum of squares at rounding level) leave no scale to estimate, and selection throws an ArgumentError; supply lambda instead. With every λⱼ fixed the criterion is only reported: where it is undefined (REML with n ≤ Mₚ or Dₚ = 0 and estimated scale, GCV with n - τ ≈ 0), criterion_value is NaN and settings.criterion_defined is false, while β, the EDF and the fitted values are still computed. sigma2 is NaN when n - τ ≈ 0 (and then so are both covariances), reml_scale when n ≤ Mₚ. Designs that do not identify the penalties' null space throw.

Fields:

  • coef: β; fitted (the means μ = h(Xβ)), residuals (response scale, y - μ), weights, linear_predictors (Xβ), deviance (D; the weighted residual sum of squares for the Gaussian family), family, link;
  • lambda, lambda_status, criterion, criterion_value;
  • edf (per coefficient, the diagonal of F = (XᵀW_F X + S_λ)⁻¹XᵀW_F X, mgcv edf), edf_total = tr(F), the trace of the influence matrix (mgcv sum(edf)), and edf_penalty, with entry j the sum of edf over the columns penalty j acts on. Penalties on the same block (a tensor product) each report that block's EDF once, undivided, so edf_penalty need not sum to edf_total; the EDF of the distinct blocks plus the unpenalized columns does;
  • sigma2: the scale mgcv reports as sig2 for every criterion: one for the binomial and Poisson families, otherwise the Pearson statistic over n - τ (D/(n - τ) for the identity link); reml_scale: Dₚ/(n - Mₚ) for the Gaussian family, one otherwise;
  • vcov_bayesian: (XᵀW_F X + S_λ)⁻¹ sigma2 (mgcv Vp); vcov_frequentist: F (XᵀW_F X + S_λ)⁻¹ sigma2 (mgcv Ve);
  • null_space_dimension (Mₚ), copied penalties and penalty_columns;
  • settings: weights source, bounds, initial values, tolerances, iterations, convergence, whether the criterion is defined (criterion_defined), final gradient and Hessian on the log scale, scale (:known or :estimated), irls_tol, irls_maxiter and the PIRLS iterations of the final fit (irls_iterations, zero for the closed-form Gaussian identity fit).

coef, vcov (type=:bayesian or :frequentist), fitted, residuals, nobs, dof (edf_total, plus one for an estimated scale), dof_residual (n - edf_total) and predict(fit, Xnew; type=:link) follow StatsAPI; type=:link (mgcv predict.gam's default) returns Xβ, type=:response the means.

Criteria

Write S_λ = Σ λ_j S_j, H = XᵀWX + S_λ, β̂ = H⁻¹XᵀWy, D = ‖W^{1/2}(y - Xβ̂)‖² (weighted residual sum of squares), Dₚ = D + β̂ᵀS_λβ̂, τ = tr(H⁻¹XᵀWX) (the trace of the influence matrix, or effective degrees of freedom), n the number of observations and Mₚ the dimension of the null space of S_λ. With the scale φ unknown:

criterionCriterion minimizedmgcv
:REML (default)`Dₚ/(2φ) + (n-Mₚ)/2 log(2πφ) + logH
:GCVn D/(n - τ)²gam(..., method="GCV.Cp")

log|S_λ|₊ is the sum of the logarithms of the positive eigenvalues. The REML default follows refund (pfr and pffr call gam with method="REML"); GCV is the criterion of fda. Profiling out φ gives the same minimizer and minimum as mgcv, which carries log φ as an extra Newton parameter.

Exponential-family responses

family=:binomial, :poisson or :gaussian with a link (canonical by default) replaces the residual sum of squares with the deviance D(β), as mgcv 1.9-3 gam(family=...):

u = mod.(0.7548776662466927 .* (1:60), 1)          # deterministic uniforms
p = 1 ./ (1 .+ exp.(-(0.5 .* z .+ 2 .* sin.(2pi .* t))))
yb = Float64.(u .< p)
logistic = StatsAPI.fit(PenalizedLeastSquares, X, yb; family=:binomial,
    penalties=[penalty(basis, 2) => 2:13])
(logistic.link, logistic.lambda, logistic.edf_total, logistic.criterion_value)

The coefficients minimize the penalized deviance Dₚ(β) = D(β) + βᵀS_λβ. Penalized IRLS (mgcv gam.fit3) takes Newton steps on Dₚ, whose Hessian is 2H with H = XᵀWₙX + S_λ and Newton weights Wₙ = D''(η)/2. For canonical links these are the Fisher weights W_F = w h'(η)²/V(μ); for other links they are mgcv's alpha-weighted ones, and a step falls back to Fisher scoring when the Newton Hessian is not positive definite (as gam.fit3 does). Steps are halved while Dₚ does not decrease or the fit leaves the family's domain. The first fit starts from R's family starting means (mustart) with one Fisher step; later fits, during smoothing parameter selection, start from the previous coefficients. Iterations stop once the Newton decrement gᵀH⁻¹g is at most irls_tol = 1e-12 times 1 + Dₚ, after which one last step is taken.

criterionCriterion minimizedmgcv
:REML (default)`Dₚ/(2φ) - ℓₛ + logH
:GCV (estimated scale)n D/(n - τ)²method="GCV.Cp"
:UBRE (known scale)D/n + 2τ/n - 1method="GCV.Cp"

ℓₛ is the saturated log-likelihood (mgcv fix.family.ls), so the REML value equals mgcv's gcv.ubre. φ = 1 for the binomial and Poisson families; for the Gaussian family φ is profiled out at Dₚ/(n - Mₚ), which gives mgcv's minimum (mgcv carries log φ as an extra Newton parameter). τ is the trace of the influence matrix computed with Fisher weights. The gradient and Hessian on log λ are exact: they include dβ/dρⱼ = -H⁻¹λⱼSⱼβ and the first and second derivatives of the weights with respect to η (Wood 2011, section 3), and the Newton search of the next section is unchanged. The criterion's Hessian at the optimum is stored in settings.hessian.

After selection, Vp = (XᵀW_F X + S_λ)⁻¹ φ̂, Ve, edf and the scale use the final Fisher weights, as mgcv's gam.fit3.post.proc; φ̂ (sigma2) is one for the binomial and Poisson families and the Pearson statistic over n - τ for the Gaussian family (mgcv's default scale.est). fitted returns the means μ, residuals the response-scale residuals y - μ, linear_predictors the linear predictor and deviance the deviance; predict(fit, X; type=:link) (mgcv's default) or type=:response. Classes that an unpenalized direction separates make the deviance decrease without bound. Where mgcv and glm warn that fitted probabilities are numerically 0 or 1 (or Poisson rates numerically 0, within 10eps), the package rejects the fit instead: such smoothing parameters are inadmissible during selection, and a fit that cannot avoid them throws, as does IRLS that does not converge within irls_maxiter iterations.

Optimization and numerical stability

Smoothing parameters are optimized on ρ = log λ by Newton's method with the exact gradient and Hessian of the criterion. The Hessian is made positive definite through the absolute values of its eigenvalues, with a floor of sqrt(eps) times the largest; steps are scaled to at most 5 on the log scale and halved up to 30 times until the criterion decreases (mgcv's newton defaults, maxNstep=5 and maxHalf=30). The starting point is mgcv's initial.sp heuristic: for each penalty, the ratio of the mean diagonal of XᵀWX to the mean diagonal of the penalty over its penalized columns, all rescaled by one power of ten so that the mean over penalized columns of diag(XᵀWX) ./ diag(H) is at least 0.4 but falls below 0.4 after a further tenfold increase. Starting values are clamped to the bounds.

Determinants, solves and traces are computed after Wood's (2011, appendix B) similarity transform. Within every group of penalties that share columns the coordinates are split into nested ranges: first the range of the penalties of largest magnitude, then, within its complement, the range of the next largest, and finally the common null space. Each transformed penalty is set exactly to zero outside its leading block, and positive definite matrices are factorized after diagonal scaling. Both the penalty log-determinant and the penalized Hessian then stay accurate when smoothing parameters differ by many orders of magnitude. Penalty ranks are judged on penalties scaled by their own norms, so Mₚ does not change with λ; rank_tol sets the relative eigenvalue threshold (default eps^(3/4)).

When the data do not identify a penalized direction, the criterion keeps decreasing as its λ_j grows (for GCV also as it shrinks towards zero), and the gradient and curvature vanish. Once the remaining gradient is below tolerance, such a parameter keeps moving in steps of 5 on the log scale while the criterion still decreases in working precision, but never beyond e^60 times its starting value. Its lambda_status is then :effectively_infinite (or :effectively_zero): further changes do not alter the fit. This is the limit λ_j → ∞, a fit constrained to the null space of S_j, computed with a large finite λ_j.

Bounds: lower and upper (on λ, not log λ) constrain each parameter; a binding bound is reported as :lower_bound or :upper_bound with λ_j exactly equal to it. Equal bounds, or the lambda keyword, fix parameters. A parameter fixed at zero drops its penalty, and with it its contribution to the penalty rank.

What is stored

Every fit stores β, the smoothing parameters and their statuses, the criterion and its value, per-coefficient and total effective degrees of freedom, the per-penalty sums of effective degrees of freedom (penalties on a shared tensor block each report that block's total once, so these sums need not add up to edf_total), sigma2, the REML scale, both covariance matrices, fitted values, residuals and weights. The settings named tuple records the bounds, initial smoothing parameters, tol, maxiter, rank_tol, Newton constants, the number of iterations, how the search ended (:gradient, :no_decrease, :maxiter or :fixed), whether the criterion is defined, and the final gradient and Hessian on the log scale. show prints all of it.

When every λ_j is fixed the criterion is only reported. Where it is undefined (REML with n ≤ M_p or a zero penalized residual sum of squares, GCV with n - τ ≈ 0) the fit still returns β, the effective degrees of freedom and the fitted values, with criterion_value = NaN and settings.criterion_defined = false. sigma2 (and with it both covariances) is NaN when n - τ ≈ 0, and the REML scale when n ≤ M_p. During selection such smoothing parameters are inadmissible.

Source audit: mgcv 1.9-3

The following was read from the mgcv 1.9-3 sources (R 4.5.1) and confirmed numerically against gam fits.

  • sig2, the scale used in Vp and Ve, is the Pearson statistic D/(n - τ) for both REML and GCV (gam.fit3.post.proc), not the REML estimate reml.scale = Dₚ/(n - Mₚ). The two coincide at a REML optimum.
  • Vp = H⁻¹ sig2; Ve = F Vp with F = H⁻¹XᵀWX; edf = diag(F).
  • The Gaussian REML score includes -Σ log wᵢ/2 for prior weights.
  • paraPen penalties are used on the supplied scale: sp multiplies the matrices as given.
  • With min.sp, the reported sp excludes the bound; the penalty multiplier is min.sp + sp. Bounds are thus approached but never attained, whereas PenalizedLeastSquares reports a binding bound exactly. mgcv has no upper bounds.
  • For exponential-family responses (gam.fit3, gdi): non-canonical links use Newton weights alpha · w h'²/V in the IRLS and in log|H| of the REML score, but the trace τ in GCV and UBRE, edf, Vp, Ve and the Pearson scale use the Fisher weights w h'²/V at the final fit; IRLS falls back to Fisher weights when the Newton system is not positive definite. The REML score adds -ls[1] (saturated log-likelihood): zero for Bernoulli data, Σ w dpois(y, y, log=TRUE) for Poisson and Σ dbinom(round(w y), round(w), y, log=TRUE) for binomial data (non-integer data are silently rounded or give -Inf; the package throws instead). With unknown scale and REML, mgcv optimizes log φ with the smoothing parameters even when all sp are fixed. Initial smoothing parameters (initial.spg) use Fisher weights at the family's mustart.
  • newton stops on a gradient tolerance of conv.tol = 1e-6 relative to the score scale. Our default tol = 1e-8 is tighter; a flat direction moves on until the criterion stops decreasing, where mgcv leaves a large finite sp.

Validation

test/penalized.jl compares fits with gam(method="REML") and gam(method="GCV.Cp") from mgcv 1.9-3 under R 4.5.1. The designs and penalties are this package's own BSplineBasis and FourierBasis matrices (test/reference/mgcv_m7_inputs.jl), passed to mgcv through paraPen: one penalty with an unpenalized covariate and a two-dimensional null space, observation weights, two penalties on disjoint blocks, two penalties on one tensor-product block, a linear effect whose smoothing parameter diverges, and a binding lower bound (min.sp). At mgcv's smoothing parameters, β, fitted values, edf, their sum, sigma2, the criterion and the upper triangle of Vp agree to relative error below 1e-8. Starting from the default, the optimizer reaches a criterion no larger than mgcv's (within 8eps). Separately, the criterion, coefficients and degrees of freedom are checked against textbook formulas, the derivatives against finite differences, and a tensor fit with smoothing parameters 1e-8 and 1e12 against 256-bit arithmetic.

To recreate the reference values:

julia --project=. test/reference/mgcv_m7_inputs.jl
Rscript test/reference/mgcv_m7.R

test/glm.jl validates penalized IRLS against mgcv 1.9-3 gam(family=..., method="REML") and method="GCV.Cp" with the same paraPen designs: Bernoulli responses (logit and probit links) and binomial proportions with trial weights, Poisson counts with one penalty and with two penalties on disjoint blocks (one of whose smoothing parameters diverges under REML), and a positive Gaussian response with the log link. At mgcv's smoothing parameters, β, the means, the linear predictor, edf, the deviance, sigma2, the REML scale, the criterion and the upper triangle of Vp agree to relative error below 1e-8; the package's own optimum is no worse than mgcv's under the criterion (within 8eps). mgcv's tolerances were tightened for the references (epsilon=1e-12, conv.tol=1e-10) so that its own fits are converged well below that tolerance. Separately, the criterion derivatives for every family and link are checked against finite differences, the solution against the penalized score equations, and the Gaussian identity link through the IRLS path against the closed-form solver. Recreate the references with

Rscript test/reference/mgcv_m7g.R

Relation to other packages

MixedModels.jl fits REML for Gaussian mixed models, but each random-effect term carries one variance parameter, so it cannot place two penalties with separate smoothing parameters on one coefficient block. The inner problem here is a linear solve (a sequence of them for penalized IRLS), and the outer problem a small smooth optimization with exact Hessians; neither needs proximal or coordinate-descent methods. GLM.jl fits unpenalized GLMs only, and is not a dependency: the same IRLS serves the unpenalized FPC GLM of FPCRegression.

Penalized splines with REML

PenalizedSpline(basis; lambda=REML()) selects one smoothing parameter for all curves with this solver. The curves are independent blocks with their own coefficients, one smoothing parameter and one noise variance, so the criterion is the REML of the stacked model. Coefficients come from the solver's stable factorization. metadata.selection stores the criterion value, λ and its status, sigma2, and for the stacked model the total effective degrees of freedom and Mₚ, as well as the initial value, tolerances, iterations and convergence. The optimizer uses the defaults above (tol=1e-8, maxiter=200, rank_tol=eps(T)^(3/4), no bounds). REML is a selector type parallel to GCV, passed through the same lambda keyword and shown in the same place. lambda remains required, and the candidate-grid GCV selector and its results are unchanged.

grid = collect(range(0.0, 1.0; length=25))
curves = GridFunctionalData([sin(2pi * s) + i * s + 0.1 * cos(13i * s) for i in 1:4, s in grid], grid)
reml = smooth_curves(curves, PenalizedSpline(BSplineBasis(4, (0.0, 1.0), 8); lambda=REML()))
reml.metadata.selection
(criterion = :REML, lambda = 2.9402440602379933e-5, status = :estimated, score = -65.97139357636914, sigma2 = 0.004723283239376528, edf = 29.620612914444052, null_space_dimension = 8, initial_lambda = 0.004610037758839587, tol = 1.0e-8, maxiter = 200, rank_tol = 1.8189894035458565e-12, iterations = 6, convergence = :gradient)
