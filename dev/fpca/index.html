Functional principal component analysis

Prefer project_scores(model, data) for score matrices and predict_scores(model, data) for a named-field ScorePrediction result. Legacy StatsAPI.predict retains the route-dependent shapes described below with deprecation warnings; see the prediction contract.

StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, data) estimates univariate functional principal components. With method=:auto, fully observed common-grid data use the dense route. Irregular and masked grid data use PACE, including designs classified as :dense_irregular. method=:pace also permits conditional-expectation scores for complete grid data. method=:dense requires complete common-grid data. Use method=:face explicitly for fast covariance smoothing on long common grids, including grids with missing observations, or for sparse curve-specific times with the sparse FACE estimator.

For GridFunctionalData, automatic routing depends on its observed-mask field at runtime, so inference includes both dense and PACE result types. Compiled callers that request a fixed method=:dense, :pace, or :face can infer that route's concrete result type. No-keyword irregular-data fits and the bandwidth candidate helper retain their inference checks.

using FunctionalDataAnalysis, Random, LinearAlgebra
import StatsAPI

grid = collect(range(0, 1; length=51))
simulation = simulate_kl(Xoshiro(42), 200;
    mean=t -> 1 + t,
    eigenfunctions=fourier_eigenfunctions((0, 1), 3),
    eigenvalues=[2.0, 0.5, 0.1], noise_sd=0,
    sampling=RegularSampling(grid))
model = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, simulation.data)
(ncomponents(model), eigvals(model), fve(model))
(3, [1.9298367758650554, 0.46521563862761356, 0.10679975551471375], [0.7713632320087722, 0.9573117241717768, 1.0])

The fit retains the sample mean, unprojected sample covariance, training scores, eigenfunctions, and the component-selection record. Dense covariance uses the denominator n - 1. Scores use the stored trapezoidal quadrature weights. Their sample covariance has the selected eigenvalues on its diagonal. The noise_variance and score_covariances accessors return nothing for a dense fit.

projected = StatsAPI.predict(model, simulation.data)
restored = reconstruct(model)
(size(projected), size(restored.values), model.selection)
((200, 3), (200, 51), (rule = :FVE, threshold = 0.99, max_k = 49, maxK = 49, chosen = 3, scores = [0.7713632320087722, 0.9573117241717768, 1.0]))

New dense curves must use exactly the training grid. Reconstruction evaluates the training mean and the piecewise-linear eigenfunctions on the requested grid, which must lie inside the training domain. The default reconstruction uses the training scores and preserves subject IDs. Other score matrices do not inherit training IDs.

scores(model) returns the fit's own score matrix. Mutating it changes the fit and subsequent reconstruction. Use copy(scores(model)) for independent storage; reconstructing from that copy does not inherit training IDs.

Prediction applies the training presmoothing settings. When spline GCV selected a penalty, prediction uses that selected penalty for new curves.

Choosing a route

The scaling benchmark compares dense FPCA with dense FACE on long grids, and PACE with sparse FACE on sparsely observed curves: three Fourier components, two noise levels and 200 replicates per cell, one CPU per fit on USC Discovery (AMD EPYC 7513, Julia 1.13.2). Dense FACE is faster from about 500 grid points and 20–200 times faster at 5000; dense FPCA is faster at 100 points. At m=5000 dense FPCA also needs about twice the memory (2.7 GB against 1.4 GB peak per process). The two routes estimate the leading eigenvalues equally well. FACE's eigenfunctions are as accurate or slightly more accurate, and its reconstructions are far closer to the noise-free curves, because dense FPCA does not smooth and FVE(0.99) then keeps tens to hundreds of noise components. Neither route failed.

On sparse curves with 2–10 points each, sparse FACE took 0.007 s (n=100) and 0.07 s (n=1000) per fit, against 0.63 s and 6.3 s for PACE. At n=1000 its eigenvalue error was less than half of PACE's (0.064 against 0.148, relative) with similar eigenfunction error, and it overestimated σ² slightly more (bias 0.045 against 0.035, true σ² 0.0875 or 0.35). At n=100 the two routes had similar eigenvalue and eigenfunction errors, but sparse FACE's σ² bias was larger (0.16 against 0.085 at the low noise level). Without a floor on the estimated noise variance, both routes sometimes failed with low noise and n=100, because the estimate was truncated to zero and the observation covariance became singular (sparse FACE 9 of 200 replicates, PACE 4 of 200), and PACE gave a few extreme reconstructions. With the estimated-noise floor described below, every replicate of every cell fits, the largest reconstruction error in the sparse cells is 0.94 (against 1.8e5 before), and eigenvalue and eigenfunction errors are unchanged; dense FACE results are identical, because the floor never binds there.

The FACE mean smoother uses a bounded basis; missing-data iterations and optional bandwidth searches add the costs described below. Automatic PACE binning reduces the covariance pool when enabled.

All tuning settings are stored on the fit and displayed by show. The implementation resolves these details left unspecified by the milestone:

  • A zero retained positive rank raises ArgumentError; FPCBasis requires at least one function. The default cap is clamped to one for two curves or two grid points, following fdapace.
  • The dense mean is stored as a one-curve GridFunctionalData; the unprojected covariance is stored as a matrix. Presmoothing metadata retains the selected GCV penalty, which is fixed for prediction.
  • Sign ties use the first largest absolute entry. The numerical tolerances are the adjustable package choices listed above.
  • Reconstruction preserves IDs when the score object is the training score matrix. A copied or different score matrix has no inferred IDs.

FACE for long common grids

FACE (Xiao, Zipunnikov, Ruppert and Crainiceanu, 2016, doi:10.1007/s11222-014-9485-x) smooths covariance through a small P-spline basis. It suits densely sampled EEG, imaging and wearable trajectories when the ordinary grid-square covariance is too large. It is an explicit route; :auto retains its dense/PACE dispatch. Sparse curve-specific times can opt into sparse FACE, described below.

model = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, data; method=:face)
fixed = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, data; method=:face, ncomponents=4,
    face=(; knots=35, p=3, m=2, lambda=2.5))
prediction = predict(model, new_grid_data)  # (scores, covariances)
recovered = reconstruct(model)
intervals = fitted_bands(model; curves=[1, 3])

The face named tuple groups settings specific to this route. Marginal multivariate FPCA accepts the same keywords. For joint MFPCA, pass univariate=(; method=:face, face=(; lambda=2.5)). Each node retains its own fit. Pooled multivariate FACE is unsupported.

For interval count k, the simple knot sequence is a + (b-a)*(-p:k+p)/k. It extends p intervals beyond both endpoints; endpoints are not clamped. The design uses the existing BSplineBasis wrapper and BSplineKit backend with this unaugmented sequence. The internal P-spline building block penalizes D_m' D_m, where D_m takes coefficient differences. This is distinct from PenalizedSpline's integrated derivative penalty. It is not exported. Require knots+p < J. Refund adds 1e-6 to all design Gram eigenvalues if the minimum is at most 1e-7. FDA throws in that case, asking for fewer knots or a smaller degree, so no ridge is added silently.

Write A=B(B'B)^(-1/2)U, where U diagonalizes the transformed difference penalty with eigenvalues s. For centered completed observations Y, let Z=A'Y' and h_j=1/(1+lambda*s_j). The GCV curve is

\[\frac{\sum_j \|Z_{j\cdot}\|^2(1-h_j)^2-\|Z\|_F^2+\|Y\|_F^2} {(1-\alpha\sum_j h_j/[J(1-f_{\rm miss})])^2}.\]

The curve is unnormalized, matching refund. FDA retains all candidates and scores even when lambda is fixed. With H=Diagonal(h)Z, the covariance is C=A*H*H'*A'/n, with denominator n, rather than dense FPCA's n-1. Fitting and scoring do not form C. For fixed basis size the main work is O(J*c^2 + n*J*c + n*c^2 + c^3), plus the bounded-dimension mean smoother and any missing-curve initialization. For selected rank K, complete matching grids share one posterior factorization per fit or prediction batch, adding O(J*K^2 + K^3 + n*J*K + n*K^2). Masked predictions retain a separate O(J_i*K^2 + K^3) factorization per curve with observed count J_i. Each missing-data pass repeats the data transformation and small eigenanalysis. The fit stores C as a J by min(n,c) factor obtained by QR of H', retaining its full surface independently of component selection. The projected covariance factor has one column per retained positive component up to maxK. covariance_data materializes a copied matrix only when requested. These are algorithmic costs, not measured benchmark results.

Mean, missing observations, and recorded choices

Refund smooths column means with stats::smooth.spline(all.knots=TRUE). FDA uses the existing cubic PenalizedSpline smoother with a bounded basis to preserve scaling on long grids and share the smoothing implementation. The basis dimension, penalty, selected lambda and full GCV curve are stored in model.settings.mean. To choose another mean smoother, set face=(; mean_smoother=PenalizedSpline(...)). A supplied PACE-style SmoothedMean or (; grid, values) is copied, retained in model.settings.mean.input, and evaluated on the data grid. Do not combine a supplied mean with a mean smoother or center=false.

Missing values initialize curve by curve with the default reduced knot count and spar GCV search of R stats 4.5.1 smooth.spline. The implementation uses the same integrated-penalty fitting and GCV algebra as smooth_curves, preparing each curve's design and matrix products once for the scalar search and final fit. Its internal initialization basis penalty preserves stats/src/sgram.f's legacy 0.3330 coefficient in place of exact 1/3; ordinary spline fits retain exact integrated penalties. Outside a curve's observed time range, refund initializes with that curve's observed centered mean. Incomplete curves need at least four observations; fully missing curves throw. Estimating a column mean needs an observed value at every grid point; a supplied mean can cover an entirely missing column. Strictly increasing times are used as supplied; FDA does not apply R's tolerance-based grouping of nearby times. Singular spline fits throw rather than replacing a curve with a constant after a failed smoothing solve.

Only the first imputation pass selects lambda. Each pass updates missing entries using refund's Euclidean component selection and shrinkage, keeping observed entries unchanged. As in the source, the stopping check compares smoothed coordinates, and missing entries are updated once more before returning. The final covariance comes from that pass's smoothed coordinates; final scores use the updated data. This ordering is retained for compatibility. The shared FVE selector uses >= throughout FDA, whereas refund uses >; FVE(1) is therefore supported. Numerical zeros are removed using eigen_tol.

model.settings records knots, degree, penalty order, candidates, GCV scores, lambda and its source, alpha, centering, mean tuning, quadrature and score rules. settings.imputation records iterations, convergence, relative changes, tolerance, cap, initial curve smoothing choices, missing fraction, and completed centered observations. A fit reaching 100 passes remains available with converged=false. show displays these settings. Original observed data and masks remain in training_data for plotting.

L² conversion, noise and shrinkage

Refund returns Euclidean-unit eigenvectors Phi_R, eigenvalues E_R of C, and scores S_R in those coordinates. Its internal Sigma=E_R/J; returned evalues=J*Sigma, efunctions=Phi_R, and scores=S_R. With rectangular weights Delta, the L² conversion is

\[\lambda=\Delta E_R,\qquad \phi=\Phi_R/\sqrt\Delta,\qquad \xi=\sqrt\Delta S_R.\]

FDA's stored trapezoidal weights are not constant, even on an equally spaced grid. They require the weighted operator sqrt(W)*C*sqrt(W), rather than separate column rescaling. A thin QR decomposition of sqrt(W)*A transfers this calculation to a c by c matrix. Nonuniform grids use the same rule. Component selection, eigenvalues, functions and scores use this L² system. Thus selected fitted curves can differ from refund's after weighting changes the eigenspaces. The reference script independently converts the covariance and solves for the corresponding scores, and also retains refund's original outputs for a rectangular compatibility calculation.

Noise remains on the observation scale:

\[\widehat\sigma^2_{\rm raw}= \operatorname{mean}_{\rm observed}(Y^2)-\operatorname{tr}(C)/J,\qquad \widehat\sigma^2=\max\{\widehat\sigma^2_{\rm raw},0.025V\},\qquad V=\max_j C_{jj}.\]

The trace and variance scale include the full smoothed covariance before truncation and are recomputed at each completion iteration. The raw estimate matches refund's mean(Y[observed]^2)-sum(Sigma); summing the L² eigenvalues without converting the integration scale would give the wrong noise variance. refund 0.1-40 fpca.face truncates at zero. The package instead uses the same positive floor as sparse FACE and PACE, so prediction from a rank-deficient subset of grid observations retains positive observation covariance. The floor and its application are recorded as described under Estimated noise and its positive floor. For selected functions Phi and diagonal L² eigenvalues Lambda, scores are

\[\widehat\xi_i=(\Phi^\mathsf{T}\Phi+\widehat\sigma^2\Lambda^{-1})^{-1} \Phi^\mathsf{T}(y_i-\mu).\]

The low-rank extension of the existing conditional-expectation solver computes this expression without a grid-square solve. With Euclidean-unit functions it reduces to refund's shrinkage by Sigma/(Sigma+sigma2/J). This is the Gaussian conditional expectation used by PACE, specialized to the selected FACE model; PACE additionally includes all components up to maxK in its observation-covariance denominator. New masked grid curves use their observed points in this posterior solve. Training predictions reproduce stored scores from completed data. Complete curves on matching grids reuse one precision factorization and posterior covariance per batch; at zero noise they reuse one pivoted QR with the same rank checks. Prediction requires exactly the training grid.

Pointwise variance: correction to refund

Under xi ~ N(0,Lambda) and independent white noise, posterior curve covariance for complete observations is

\[V=\widehat\sigma^2\Phi (\Phi^\mathsf{T}\Phi+\widehat\sigma^2\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}\Phi^\mathsf{T}.\]

fitted_bands uses diag(V), consistent with shrinkage scores and the package's conditional-variance contract. In refund 0.1-40, the var=TRUE code instead inserts its internal Diagonal(Sigma) in place of the inverse score covariance. In its returned coordinates Sigma=E_R/J; this also introduces a scale inconsistency. For the default reference fixture's first grid point, the weighted posterior variance is approximately 0.01874790. Replacing Lambda^{-1} by Lambda in the same weighted formula gives 0.01971711. The test also preserves and verifies refund's actual unconverted diag.var (approximately 0.01906664 at this point), and shows that it differs from the corresponding posterior.

The mean, covariance, selected rank and noise are treated as fixed. Training missing values are treated as completed observations, matching refund's design; imputation uncertainty is excluded. coverage=:interval supports custom evaluation grids and curve subsets through the existing band layer. Simultaneous FACE bands are unsupported; coverage=:band throws.

Reference values are recreated with Rscript test/reference/refund_m6.R, using refund 0.1-40 and R 4.5.1. The committed fixtures have 40 curves on 50 points, with default lambda selection, fixed lambda 2.5, missing values, and an independent calculation of FDA's mean-smoother departure. Tests compare means, spectra, eigenfunctions, scores, noise, lambda, GCV curves, completed data and five fitted curves at relative error below 1e-8. The fixtures retain all eigenfunctions and scores. Variance is checked against an independent posterior calculation, with the refund discrepancy kept visible. CI reads the CSVs and does not require R.

Sparse FACE

For irregular observations, method=:face implements Xiao, Li, Checkley and Crainiceanu (2018), doi:10.1007/s11222-017-9744-8. :auto continues to choose PACE. The reference implementation is face 0.1-8: face.sparse.inner.r, pspline.r, pspline.setting, raw.construct, select.knots, and predict.face.sparse. Sparse FACE fits a symmetric tensor-product P-spline covariance and a constant measurement-error variance jointly from within-subject residual products, including the diagonal. This is a different estimator from the sandwich smoother for common grids.

cd4 = load_dataset(:cd4).data

# Both use the same grid and quadrature; align signs when comparing functions.

for k in axes(pace_phi, 2)
    if sum(sparse_face.weights .* face_phi[:, k] .* pace_phi[:, k]) < 0
        pace_phi[:, k] .*= -1
    end
end

What the reference calls gcv

Write y_i for a subject's vector of upper-triangular residual products, including its squared observations. Let X be the symmetric tensor-product design augmented by the diagonal indicator. The covariance penalty is

\[Q=\operatorname{blockdiag}\{G^\top(I_c\otimes D_m^\top D_m)G,0\},\]

where G duplicates the lower triangle into a symmetric coefficient matrix. The reference diagonalizes the ridge-adjusted Gram matrix and penalty to obtain coordinates A=X A_0 and penalty spectrum s. For d_j=(1+lambda*s_j)^(-1), its smoothing matrix is S=A*Diagonal(d)*A'. With r=y-S*y, its actual criterion is

\[\|r\|^2-\|y\|^2+2\sum_i r_i^\top S_{ii}r_i.\]

This is the first-order approximation to leave-one-subject-out squared prediction error: replace (I-S_ii)^(-1) by I+S_ii in the deleted-subject residual and retain the first-order term. It is neither exact deletion nor the usual trace-denominator GCV. The omitted y'y is constant across lambda. The mean uses the same criterion with S=A*Diagonal(d)*A'*W, where each subject's weights are 1/N_i; squared prediction errors remain unweighted. The covariance uses identity weights on the unique within-subject pairs.

The fitted symmetric coefficient matrix is projected to its positive semidefinite part before weighted functional eigenanalysis. The known m*(m+1)/2+1 null penalty directions are set to zero numerically; their count is stored as penalty_nullity. Normal matrices and their decompositions use at least Float64 precision before converting coordinates to the input floating type; normal_matrix_type records that precision. Only spline-sized matrices are diagonalized. The covariance is stored as a grid-by-rank factor; plotting or requesting Matrix(model.covariance) materializes the surface. For P=sum_i N_i*(N_i+1)/2 and q=c*(c+1)/2+1, the tensor design uses O(P*q) storage; normal-matrix formation takes O(P*q^2) and decomposition takes O(q^3). This avoids a grid-square covariance during fitting, but subjects with many observations still contribute quadratically many raw products.

Scale, scores, prediction, and uncertainty

R reports functional eigenvalues using the average-grid metric B'B/J. With constant quadrature weight w, convert its eigenvalues by J*w, eigenfunctions by 1/sqrt(J*w), and scores by sqrt(J*w). With trapezoidal or nonuniform weights, diagonalize the small weighted operator instead; a single scalar conversion is insufficient. Noise stays on the measurement scale. The tests independently perform this conversion from R's fitted coefficient covariance.

At arbitrary training or prediction observation times, the fitted spline mean, eigenfunctions, and full positive coefficient covariance are evaluated exactly. For selected eigenfunctions Phi_i, selected eigenvalues Lambda, and full spline covariance C_i, the shared conditional-expectation solver computes

\[\widehat\xi_i=\Lambda\Phi_i^\top(C_i+\sigma^2 I)^{-1}(y_i-\mu_i),\qquad \Omega_i=\Lambda-\Lambda\Phi_i^\top(C_i+\sigma^2 I)^{-1}\Phi_i\Lambda.\]

This is the coefficient BLUP in predict.face.sparse, expressed in the weighted functional eigenbasis. All positive spline directions enter C_i, including directions beyond maxK or the selected K. New grid or irregular data must lie within the work grid. The score solve uses the fitted positive noise variance unchanged; prediction applies no additional ridge or floor. predict returns the usual (scores,covariances) tuple. Reconstruction and pointwise fitted_bands use the selected components, with variance diag(Phi*Omega_i*Phi'); omitted components and estimation uncertainty are excluded. R's direct curve predictions and standard errors use its full coefficient covariance, irrespective of pve; they coincide with this projection when all positive components are retained. Reconstruction on a new grid uses the shared piecewise-linear FPCBasis interface.

Reference values are recreated with Rscript test/reference/face_m6.R using face 0.1-8, refund 0.1-40, mgcv 1.9-3, Matrix 1.7-3, and R 4.5.1. Fixtures cover the milestone-3 sparse inputs and all 366 CD4 subjects (1,888 observed counts), exported with the same native months, positional IDs, and missing-cell convention as load_dataset(:cd4). Default selections and fixed covariance lambda 2.5 / mean lambda 0.75 are checked. Mean curves, weighted spectra, eigenfunctions, noise, tuning, both criterion curves, scores, their covariances, and selected fitted values and variances are compared at relative error below 1e-8. R's full coefficient predictions and standard errors are also checked after retaining all positive components. The CD4 fixture keeps score and posterior outputs for a small set of subjects, including a singleton; it does not store full covariance surfaces. CI consumes CSVs without R.

PACE for sparse observations

PACE estimates a pooled mean and latent covariance, then predicts each subject's principal component scores by conditional expectation. It reuses SmoothedMean and SmoothedCovariance. A no-keyword fit uses the work grid, Gaussian kernel, and fractional bandwidths listed above. The Gaussian avoids empty windows caused by compact support, but it cannot prevent ill-conditioned local fits on extremely coarse or clustered data. Such fits raise an informative error suggesting a larger bandwidth. Increase mean_bandwidth or covariance_bandwidth explicitly when needed; the fit does not enlarge them automatically.

Estimated noise and its positive floor

PACE and both FACE routes use noise_variance(model) = max(raw_noise_variance, 0.025V) when noise is estimated. The scale V is a maximum fitted pointwise signal variance:

  • PACE uses the diagonal of the full positive projected covariance retained up to maxK, evaluated on the work grid. Its piecewise-linear representation also bounds the fitted variance between grid points.
  • Sparse FACE uses the full positive spline covariance on the work grid and at every training observation time, including components beyond the selected K.
  • Dense FACE uses the full smoothed covariance on the common grid at each completion iteration, before selecting components.

This scale has the same squared observation units as the raw estimate. Multiplying observations and their fitted mean by a multiplies both the raw variance and the floor by a², preserving whether the floor binds. Changing time units leaves the pointwise covariance scale unchanged. Using functional eigenvalues without their integration scale would not have this time-unit invariance. Explicit noise_variance values on the PACE route, including zero, are used unchanged. Noise inherited from a supplied SmoothedCovariance is still estimated and receives the floor. Standalone SmoothedCovariance continues to truncate negative estimates to zero; the FPCA fit retains its raw estimate before that truncation.

The factor 0.025 controls statistical amplification as well as numerical conditioning. Let K_i be the positive-semidefinite fitted observation covariance for a curve with m_i observations and diagonal entries at most V. Its largest eigenvalue is at most m_i*V, so

\[\kappa_2(K_i+\sigma^2 I) \le 1+\frac{m_iV}{\sigma^2} \le 1+40m_i.\]

For at most ten observations this bound is 401. At a target with fitted variance at most V, the map from the observation residual vector to the conditional reconstructed deviation has Euclidean gain at most sqrt(10). To see this, factor the full fitted covariance as BB'. The singular values of B'*(BB'+sigma2*I)^(-1) are s/(s²+sigma2), bounded by 1/(2sqrt(sigma2)); multiplying by a target factor row with norm at most sqrt(V) gives the bound. Restricting the reconstructed numerator to selected components preserves it. These bounds motivate the constant as a safeguard against amplification of noisy observations; they do not bound reconstruction error against an unknown true curve. Sparse FACE evaluates splines exactly, so an unseen prediction time can have fitted variance above the stored V. The same stored noise is used there, but the uniform sqrt(10) gain bound then does not apply. Prediction does not silently change the fit's floor.

The source audit found that face 0.1-8 face.sparse.inner resets estimates at or below 1e-6 to 1e-6, and predict.face.sparse uses max(sigma2, 1e-6). fdapace 0.6.0 GetSmoothedCovarSurface replaces strictly negative estimates by 1e-6 but leaves zero and tiny positive estimates unchanged. Dense FACE's reference, refund 0.1-40 fpca.face, truncates at zero. The relative floor deliberately departs from these safeguards: an absolute threshold changes its relative strength with the observation units and can leave conditional estimates highly sensitive to noise. fdapace's optional rho path is prior art for replacing the estimated variance when scoring needs more regularization. Its GetRho selects a value using reconstructed total variance. The package uses the fixed relative rule above and does not reproduce that selector or its default methodRho="vanilla" behavior.

The fit records settings.raw_noise_variance, settings.noise_floor, settings.noise_floor_relative, settings.noise_floor_scale, settings.noise_floor_scale_source, settings.noise_floor_applied, settings.noise_variance_source, and settings.noise_truncation; show displays them. For estimated noise, noise_floor=0.025V, noise_floor_relative=0.025, noise_floor_scale=V, and noise_floor_scale_source=:maximum_fitted_pointwise_variance. noise_floor_applied tests whether the raw estimate is strictly below the threshold, and noise_truncation=:positive_floor. Equality leaves the estimate unchanged and the flag false. For supplied noise, the threshold and three scale fields are nothing, the flag is false, and noise_truncation=:none; raw_noise_variance records the supplied value. The existing noise_variance accessor returns the variance actually used in component selection, scores, uncertainties, reconstruction, and prediction. Prediction and derivative scores reuse this stored value without adding a second floor.

Let $\widetilde G$ denote the positive-semidefinite projection of the work-grid surface over all positive components retained up to max_k. Negative eigenvalues are dropped and reported in model.dropped. The selected component count $K$ can be smaller than this retained rank. At subject $i$'s observed times, let $\Phi_i$ contain the selected eigenfunctions and $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_K)$. The scores and their conditional covariance are

\[\begin{aligned} \Sigma_i &= \widetilde G(t_i,t_i)+\sigma^2 I,\\ \widehat\xi_i &= \Lambda\Phi_i^\mathsf T\Sigma_i^{-1}(y_i-\mu_i),\\ \Omega_i &= \Lambda-\Lambda\Phi_i^\mathsf T\Sigma_i^{-1}\Phi_i\Lambda. \end{aligned}\]

The denominator $\Sigma_i$ uses the full retained projected covariance, including components beyond the selected $K$. This is the observed-curve covariance in Yao, Müller, and Wang (2005) and fdapace. The numerator and $\Omega_i$ use the selected components. Mean and eigenfunction evaluation are linear; covariance evaluation is bilinear. The systems are solved by Cholesky factorization, using the recorded positive floor when noise is estimated. No additional ridge or pseudoinverse is added. A non-positive-definite system, including one from explicit zero noise, raises an error naming the curve and suggesting an explicit noise_variance.

score_covariances(model) is a K × K × n array: slice [:, :, i] is $\Omega_i$. Under the Gaussian model, it measures remaining uncertainty about the latent scores after observing curve $i$. It is not the sample covariance of the predicted scores. Use it when propagating score uncertainty to reconstructions or downstream inference; sparse-curve scores can have substantial shrinkage and uncertainty. These covariances condition on the fitted mean, covariance, and noise variance, so they do not include uncertainty from estimating those quantities.

new_scores, new_covariances = StatsAPI.predict(model, simulation.data[1:3])
restored = reconstruct(model)

PACE prediction accepts grid or irregular data and returns (scores, covariances). It uses the training mean, eigenfunctions, projected covariance, and noise variance. Every observation time must lie inside the work-grid domain. Prediction on the training data agrees with the stored scores and conditional covariances. Supplied means, surfaces, and noise variance

Supply mean=smoothed_mean to reuse a SmoothedMean, or mean=(; grid, values) for an explicit mean curve. The mean grid must cover the entire work grid and every observation time. Supply covariance=smoothed_covariance to reuse a SmoothedCovariance, or covariance=(; grid, surface) for a finite symmetric covariance surface. Surfaces that are asymmetric only by rounding (relative tolerance sqrt(eps), as R's isSymmetric allows) are accepted and replaced by (S + S')/2. The surface grid must cover the work grid. A supplied surface requires an explicit nonnegative noise_variance. Supplied objects are copied with their settings; they are not refitted.

The fit stores its work-grid mean as one-curve GridFunctionalData and its unprojected work-grid covariance as a matrix. settings.mean_input and settings.covariance_input retain copied supplied or estimated inputs; settings.full_projected_covariance retains the surface used for scores and prediction. Mean, covariance, and noise provenance are recorded separately. Noise inherited from a SmoothedCovariance retains :estimated provenance, including when that covariance object was supplied to the fit.

If the covariance must be estimated, the mean must be estimated in the same fit or supplied as a SmoothedMean. A named-tuple mean cannot be passed to the existing pooled covariance smoother; supply a covariance surface with that mean instead. Bandwidth defaults are used only for the respective estimated inputs. Supplying mean_bandwidth together with a supplied mean raises ArgumentError. Supplying covariance_bandwidth or diagonal_bandwidth together with a supplied covariance also raises ArgumentError. Reused inputs retain their fitted tuning values; unused bandwidth overrides are rejected.

Binning only affects covariance estimation. With a supplied covariance, binning must remain :auto, and the fit records settings.binning=nothing and settings.binning_rule=:not_applicable. When both mean and covariance are supplied, kernel must remain nothing and weighting must remain :obs; overrides raise ArgumentError. Their active settings are recorded as nothing because no smoother runs. If either input is estimated, kernel and weighting still tune that smoother. Supplied fitted inputs keep their original settings under settings.mean_input and settings.covariance_input.

Overriding defaults and selecting bandwidths

Every automatic choice can be replaced explicitly. For example:

import KernelSmoothing

model = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, simulation.data;
    method=:pace,
    grid=collect(range(extrema(simulation.data.times)...; length=61)),
    ncomponents=2, max_k=6,
    kernel=KernelSmoothing.GaussianKernel(), weighting=:subj,
    mean_bandwidth=0.15, covariance_bandwidth=0.25,
    diagonal_bandwidth=0.20, binning=nothing)

For an irregular object, use extrema(simulation.data.times) to obtain the observed endpoints: concatenated times are ordered within curves, rather than across curves. A work grid may cover a wider interval, but all observations must remain inside it. Fractional bandwidth defaults always use the observed time range, not the work-grid range.

pace_bandwidth_candidates returns the ten geometric mean and covariance bandwidths from fdapace's GCVLwls1D1 and GCVLwls2DV2 candidate grids. The candidate values use the Gaussian convention of the PACE default. Pass kernel=KernelSmoothing.EpanechnikovKernel() to generate the compact-kernel variant, and use the same kernel for fit. The helper does not run fdapace's GCV, choose a bandwidth, or fit a smoother. Use whole-curve CV to select from these candidates:

candidates = pace_bandwidth_candidates(simulation.data)
model = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, simulation.data;
    mean_bandwidth=CurveCV(candidates.mean; folds=10, rng=Xoshiro(44)),
    covariance_bandwidth=CurveCV(candidates.covariance; folds=10, rng=Xoshiro(45)))

Pass grid=work_grid to both the helper and fit when using a custom work grid. CurveCV retains the candidate grid, errors, and fold assignments. Mean selectors and covariance selectors pass to the existing pooled smoothers unchanged. The 2D covariance smoother accepts a number or explicit CurveCV candidates; KernelSmoothing bandwidth selectors are univariate. Covariance CV holds the supplied fitted mean fixed across folds. It holds out whole curves, whereas fdapace's covariance CV holds out raw covariance pairs. These procedures therefore need not select the same bandwidth. The helper requires enough unique observation times and time pairs for the audited nearest-neighbor starts. It throws if they are unavailable. Equal candidate endpoints can yield repeated values; use unique(candidates.mean) or unique(candidates.covariance) before CurveCV in that case.

Cost and automatic binning

Let N_i be the number of observations on curve i, M=sum(N_i), P=sum(N_i*(N_i-1)), and g the work-grid size. Fixed-bandwidth pooled mean smoothing costs O(g*M); unbinned covariance smoothing costs O(g^2*P) after forming P off-diagonal products. The weighted eigenstep costs O(g^3) and the per-curve Cholesky score solves cost O(sum(N_i^3)). The defaults perform no bandwidth search.

For binning=:auto, take m=median(N_i) for sparse designs and m=max(N_i) otherwise, as in fdapace's audited rule:

  1. With m<=20, do not bin.
  2. With m>400, initially choose 400 bins.
  3. With n>5000, compute mstar=max(20,400+19*(5000-n)/2250) and choose ceil(mstar) bins if mstar<m; otherwise do not bin.
  4. If none of these rules selects a count, do not bin.

The candidate helper and bin-count decision use fdapace's IsRegular classification. Let u be the number of unique pooled observation times and coverage=M/(n*u). Full coverage is Dense with at least eight unique times and Sparse with fewer than eight. Incomplete coverage above 0.8 is DenseWithMV; other designs are Sparse. This classifier controls only the mean-candidate start and bin-count statistic. It does not change FPCA's :auto route: complete GridFunctionalData use dense FPCA, and irregular or masked data use PACE.

The resolved bin count and the decision's design, count statistic, summary count, and number of curves are stored and shown. Binning snaps covariance-pool times and combines within-curve cell products with their original weights; it does not discard observations. It uses the existing covariance smoother, rather than fdapace's separate preprocessing of each curve. The pooled mean and work-grid length are unchanged by this aggregation. With b bins, Q=sum(min(N_i*(N_i-1),b^2)) bounds its occupied cells. Exact duplicate pair coordinates are also combined across subjects for a fixed-bandwidth fit, without moving times or combining the original diagonal observations. Thus O(g^2*Q) smoothing and O(Q) pair storage remain upper bounds, after O(P) work to form the products. Most sparse curves have at most 20 observations, so automatic binning does not turn on, even when n is large.

The candidate helper's covariance nearest-neighbor calculation itself takes O(U^2) time and O(U) storage for U unique time pairs, including diagonal pairs. It is optional and is not run by a default fit.

A 2D CurveCV with H candidate bandwidths and F folds splits subjects before combining exact duplicate coordinates separately in training and held-out pools. Per candidate and fold, smoothing costs the product of those pools' distinct-coordinate counts. Without duplicates, the bounds remain O(H*P^2 + H*F*P) without binning, or O(H*Q^2 + H*F*Q) with binning. The held-out products are evaluated at their pool coordinates (snapped when binning is enabled), rather than only at the work grid. Ten candidate bandwidths can therefore cost much more than a fixed-bandwidth fit. Reduce the candidate set or set an explicit bin count when that expense is inappropriate. See Smoothing for the weight-preserving binning and CV contracts.

PACE component selection

Missing mean and covariance inputs estimate the pooled smoothers, following Yao, Müller, and Wang (2005). Missing noise_variance uses SmoothedCovariance's raw estimate with the positive floor; a supplied surface requires an explicit number. The score solve uses this stored noise variance and must admit Cholesky factorization. Eigenanalysis, signs, and numerical tolerances are shared with the dense route.

For AIC() and BIC(), each candidate $k$ uses its own $k$-component covariance plus $\sigma^2 I$ in the Gaussian likelihood. The criterion is minus twice the log likelihood plus $2k$ or $k\log(n)$, respectively. selection.scores stores the full criterion curve for k=1:max_k; selection.candidates records those counts. When max_k exceeds the retained positive rank, the additional components have zero variance, leave the likelihood unchanged, and increase only the penalty. Selection stops at the first increase, following fdapace. The chosen count and selection.global_minimizer are both recorded, so a later lower value remains visible. The Gaussian normalization constant is included; fdapace omits it, which does not affect either selected count.

The analytic tests check the conditional-expectation formulas, including the full-covariance denominator. A separate 3000-curve sparse Gaussian simulation uses known mean, covariance, and noise with 3 to 8 points per curve. Mean squared score error is compared with the mean trace of $\Omega_i$ within 10%, and each standardized component error variance is checked within 10% of one. These are Monte Carlo checks, not coverage guarantees after smoothing.

Default recovery checks and their limits

A fixed-seed simulate_kl check uses 400 sparse curves, 2–6 observations per curve, Fourier constant/sine components with eigenvalues [2.0,0.7], and noise variance 1.0. A no-keyword fit has weighted mean L2 error below 0.30, sign-invariant weighted L2 error below 0.35 for each of the first two eigenfunctions, and noise relative error below 0.40. An 80-curve, 25-point regular-grid check compares dense and PACE eigenvalues within 15% and absolute weighted eigenfunction correlations above 0.98.

These thresholds describe finite simulations. They are not universal recovery guarantees. A historical lower-noise diagnostic with the same sparse signal and true variance 0.25 estimated 0.56615 under the former surface-diagonal, middle-half default. That value does not describe the current rotated, full-interval default, chosen using the Discovery results. The fixed 0.10r covariance bandwidth can leave appreciable smoothing bias. Inspect the fitted noise estimate and use an explicit bandwidth selector or known noise_variance when appropriate.

Basis and numerical conventions

eigenfunctions(model) is an FPCBasis. Its columns are orthonormal under the stored work-grid quadrature weights. Evaluation between work-grid points uses linear interpolation. Evaluation outside the domain throws. gram(basis) instead integrates the products of those piecewise-linear interpolants exactly. It is generally close to, but not exactly, the identity. Derivative evaluation and positive-order roughness penalties are unsupported.

The weighted eigenproblem is sqrt(W) * covariance * sqrt(W), where W=Diagonal(weights). Dividing its eigenvectors by sqrt.(weights) gives work-grid eigenfunctions. See Comparison with fdapace for source-level details and the explicit compatibility convention used by the R golden tests. Spectrum sums and sign comparisons are evaluated after rescaling to avoid overflow while preserving the stated FVE and relative sign rules.

The dense covariance denominator differs from the n denominator in the FunctionalGraphicalModels.jl reference. The multivariate comparison below accounts for this convention explicitly.

Multivariate FPCA

StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, mfd::MultivariateFunctionalData) returns a MultivariateFPCA. The default basis=:marginal fits each node with the univariate method above. Nodes may use different grids or mix complete grid data with sparse irregular data. Each node retains its own route, defaults, tuning record, and conditional score covariances, when applicable. This operation performs separate marginal decompositions. The joint multivariate decomposition of Happ and Greven is a later milestone 4 task.

model = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, mfd; basis=:marginal, ncomponents=2)
node_model = component(model, 1)
node_scores = scores(model, 1)
counts = ncomponents(model) # one selected count per node

For marginal fitting, every univariate keyword accepts either a shared value or a length-p vector containing one value per node. A numeric work grid is one shared grid; an outer vector of grids or nothing entries specifies one choice per node. To supply different means or covariance surfaces, use a vector of the same fitted objects or named tuples accepted by univariate FPCA. A vector with the wrong node count throws. For example:

model = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, mfd;
    basis=:marginal, ncomponents=[2, 3], max_k=[4, 5],
    grid=[work_grid_node_1, work_grid_node_2])

The basis=:pooled route estimates one common basis from the average of the node sample covariance matrices. It follows the partial-separability construction of Zapata, Oh, and Petersen (2022). Every node must be complete GridFunctionalData on exactly the same grid. Masked grids, irregular nodes, and mismatched grids throw. Pooled PACE is not implemented.

For centered node matrices $Z_j=X_j-\mathbf 1\widehat\mu_j^\mathsf T$, the pooled surface and node scores are

\[\widehat G_{\mathrm{pool}} =\frac1p\sum_{j=1}^p\frac{Z_j^\mathsf T Z_j}{n-1}, \qquad \widehat\xi_{ijm} =\sum_g w_g\{X_{ij}(t_g)-\widehat\mu_j(t_g)\}\widehat\phi_m(t_g).\]

The weighted eigenstep and positive-eigenvalue cutoff are the same as for univariate FPCA. ncomponents is an integer or FVE(threshold), defaulting to FVE(0.99); max_k follows the dense route. Selection uses the pooled spectrum. The sign rule uses the average of the node mean curves as its common reference and records sign_reference=:average_node_mean. Near-zero overlap uses the same largest-absolute-entry fallback as univariate FPCA. The pooled tuning keywords are ncomponents, max_k, eigen_tol, and sign_tol; method=:auto or :dense is accepted. Pooled presmoothing, custom work grids, and PACE smoothing options throw because no corresponding pooled method is implemented. Each node keeps its own sample mean, and eigvals(model)[j] reports that node's score sample variances, with denominator n-1. These node variances need not be ordered within a node because the shared basis is ordered by the pooled spectrum. eigenfunctions(model) returns the shared FPCBasis for every node. component(model, j) throws for a pooled fit because there is no separately fitted univariate FPCA at that node.

pooled = StatsAPI.fit(FPCA, mfd; basis=:pooled, ncomponents=2)

Unequal counts throw and suggest an integer ncomponents; scores are never padded or truncated. scores(model) always returns a vector of node score matrices. These matrices are the fit's own storage; copy them before independent mutation. score_covariances(model) returns a vector whose entries are conditional covariance arrays for PACE nodes and nothing for dense or pooled nodes.

StatsAPI.predict(model, newmfd) returns one prediction per node using the training means and bases. Dense predictions are score matrices; PACE predictions are the univariate (scores, covariances) named tuples. reconstruct(model) returns MultivariateFunctionalData and preserves training subject IDs. Fully missing curves throw; incomplete curves need four observations for initialization. Estimating a mean requires at least one observation per grid column. Iterations, convergence, changes, initialization tuning, and completed centered data are stored. Training scores and uncertainties use the completed data as refund does.

FACE covariance storage is low rank; fitting never forms the grid-square covariance. Weighted eigenanalysis gives the same stored-quadrature L² scale as dense/PACE. On rectangular weights Δ, refund's eigenvalues, eigenfunctions, and scores convert by Δ, 1/sqrt(Δ), and sqrt(Δ), respectively; trapezoidal weights require diagonalizing the small weighted operator. Raw noise variance is mean(observed_centered_y²) - tr(smoothed_covariance)/grid_length on the measurement scale. Each completion iteration uses max(raw_noise_variance, 0.025V), where V is the maximum diagonal of its full smoothed covariance on the grid. This relative floor departs from refund's zero truncation to stabilize conditional scores, including partial predictions. Scores use Gaussian shrinkage under the selected covariance, computed by the low-rank conditional-expectation solver. FACE fitted_bands provides pointwise posterior variance consistent with those scores, correcting refund's use of eigenvalues in place of their inverses. Simultaneous FACE bands are unsupported. See the FPCA documentation for the source audit and exact scale and variance formulas.

For IrregularFunctionalData, method=:face fits sparse FACE (Xiao, Li, Checkley and Crainiceanu, 2018), following face 0.1-8 face.sparse. :auto still selects PACE. Sparse face defaults are knots=7 equal intervals (six strictly interior knots), p=3, m=2, center=true, lambda=nothing, and loglambda=range(-3,10; length=14). Knots span the observed range expanded by 0.1% at each end. grid=nothing uses 100 equally spaced points across the observed range; an override must cover all observations and stay within that range, with full spline rank. max_k defaults to knots+p. Sparse face.lambda_mean fixes the mean penalty and face.mean_loglambda sets its log-candidate grid; these are independent of covariance face.lambda/face.loglambda. Dense FACE instead accepts a face.mean_smoother object. These different mean smoothers are not aliases. The reference's gcv approximates leave-one-subject-out squared prediction error to first order, omitting the constant sum of squared raw products. It fits the covariance and noise jointly, including diagonal raw products. Its explicit covariance ridge is 1e-6*maximum(eigvals(X'X)), recorded in settings.covariance_ridge. Estimated noise is max(raw_noise_variance, 0.025V), where V is the maximum fitted variance of the full positive spline covariance over the work grid and all training observation times. This scale-invariant floor deliberately replaces face 0.1-8's absolute 1e-6 threshold.

Sparse FACE's estimated mean uses the shared P-spline building block with the same interval count, cubic degree, second differences, and observation weights 1/N_i. Its independent lambda_mean=nothing selects among exp.(mean_loglambda), where mean_loglambda=range(-20,20; length=100); these defaults come from face's pspline. A fixed lambda_mean or a supplied PACE-style mean is accepted. The mean's reference ridge rule adds 1e-6 when its Gram matrix has an eigenvalue at most 1e-7; its value is stored. Sparse scores use exact spline evaluations and the full positive spline covariance in the shared conditional-expectation solver, as in predict.face.sparse. Both data representations can be predicted inside the work grid. Pointwise fitted_bands describe the selected-component posterior. FVE consistently uses weighted functional eigenvalues; face's component count instead uses coefficient-space eigenvalues. Non-default m consistently means the requested difference order; face's covariance penalty is always second order despite its m argument. two_step is unsupported and throws. All grids, criteria, ridges, signs, and component choices are stored and shown.

PACE implements the conditional-expectation method of Yao, Müller, and Wang (2005). Its work grid defaults to 51 equally spaced points over the observed time range (fdapace 0.6.0 nRegGrid, SetOptions.R). An override must cover every observation time. A missing mean estimates SmoothedMean; a supplied SmoothedMean must have derivative=0 and is copied, or supply (; grid, values) for a linearly interpolated mean. Its grid must cover the work grid and every observation. A missing covariance estimates SmoothedCovariance, using an estimated or supplied SmoothedMean. Alternatively supply that fitted type or a symmetric (; grid, surface) for bilinear interpolation. noise_variance=nothing uses max(raw_noise_variance, 0.025V) from the covariance smoother; a supplied surface requires a finite nonnegative override. Here V is the maximum diagonal of the full positive projected covariance retained up to maxK on the work grid. PACE and both FACE routes use this relative floor to stabilize conditional-expectation systems. It deliberately departs from fdapace 0.6.0's negative-only replacement by 1e-6, face 0.1-8's absolute 1e-6 floor, and refund 0.1-40's zero truncation. Multiplying observation units by a multiplies both the raw variance and floor by a²; changing time units does not change their ratio. fdapace's optional rho regularization is prior art for stabilizing conditional scores, but this fixed rule does not reproduce its selector. The factor 0.025 bounds the observation-covariance condition number by 1+40m for m observations whose fitted variances are at most V. At a target with fitted variance at most V, the Euclidean gain from the observation residual vector to the conditional reconstructed deviation is at most sqrt(10). Sparse spline predictions at unseen times can have larger fitted variance and are not covered by that uniform bound. A supplied noise_variance, including zero, is used unchanged. The fit stores settings.raw_noise_variance before zero truncation, settings.noise_floor (0.025V when estimated, nothing when supplied), settings.noise_floor_relative (0.025), settings.noise_floor_scale (V), and settings.noise_floor_scale_source (:maximum_fitted_pointwise_variance). These three scale fields are nothing for supplied noise. settings.noise_floor_applied is true only when the raw estimate is below the floor. settings.noise_variance_source and settings.noise_truncation (:positive_floor when estimated, :none when supplied) retain provenance. These settings are shown with the fit; noise_variance returns the value used for scoring and prediction. Standalone SmoothedCovariance retains its zero truncation. Supplying a fitted mean together with mean_bandwidth, or a covariance with covariance_bandwidth or diagonal_bandwidth, throws rather than ignoring the requested tuning of an input that is not refitted. With a supplied covariance, binning must remain :auto; the fit records binning=nothing and binning_rule=:not_applicable. When both inputs are supplied, kernel must remain nothing and weighting must remain :obs; their active settings are recorded as nothing because no smoother runs. Supplied fitted inputs retain their original smoothing settings.

PACE uses local linear degree one (Yao, Müller, and Wang, 2005). kernel=nothing resolves to Gaussian, and weighting=:obs follows fdapace 0.6.0 SetOptions.R; :subj is also available (neither scheme uniformly dominates: Zhang and Wang, 2016, doi:10.1214/16-AOS1446). The standalone smoothers retain Epanechnikov. mean_bandwidth=nothing and covariance_bandwidth=nothing resolve respectively to 0.05 and 0.10 times the observed time range (fdapace methodBwMu="Default", methodBwCov="Default"). Explicit numbers, CurveCV, and mean KernelSmoothing selectors pass unchanged to the existing smoothers. diagonal_bandwidth=nothing uses the selected covariance bandwidth, following fdapace and milestone 2; override it with a number or univariate selector. pace_bandwidth_candidates supplies fdapace's optional candidate grids.

binning=:auto follows fdapace GetBinNum: use the median curve count for fdapace's Sparse classification and the maximum otherwise. Classification uses IsRegular's pooled observed-time coverage, as described in pace_bandwidth_candidates, independently of the FPCA route. Counts at most 20 never bin; counts above 400 initially use 400 bins. If n>5000, set mstar=max(20,400+19*(5000-n)/2250) and use ceil(mstar) bins only when the unrounded mstar is below the count. Otherwise no bins are selected. Override with nothing or an integer >=2. The selected count is passed to the existing covariance smoother, which preserves raw pair weights and snaps covariance/diagonal training times; the mean and output grid are unchanged. This differs from fdapace's raw-curve averaging and possible output-grid reduction. Both the request and resolved count are stored. With P=sum(N_i*(N_i-1)) pairs and M=length(grid), fixed covariance smoothing costs O(M^2*P) time. A 2D CurveCV with H candidates costs O(H*P^2 + H*F*P) for F folds, before the final fit; binning reduces P to the number of occupied per-curve cells (at most n*binning^2). max_k=nothing resolves to max(1,min(20,length(grid)-2,nobs(fd)-2)) for PACE, following fdapace 0.6.0's smoothed route. An explicit PACE cap may be at most length(grid).

The full positive-semidefinite covariance projection up to max_k is used in each curve's Cholesky score solve. The selected eigenvalues and eigenfunctions define the score numerator and conditional covariance. PACE also supports AIC and BIC. Their criteria use the K-component covariance, including the Gaussian normalization constant, for every candidate K=1:max_k. Beyond the retained positive rank the covariance has zero additional variance, while the penalty still increases. Selection stops at the first increase as fdapace does; the global minimizer is retained too. Scoring adds no ridge beyond the recorded estimated-noise floor. A non-positive-definite observation covariance throws with the curve index and a suggestion to supply noise_variance.

The eigensystem of sqrt(W)*G*sqrt(W) uses the data's stored trapezoidal weights, including on nonuniform grids. Eigenvalues must exceed eigen_tol times the largest absolute eigenvalue. The relative cutoff 1e-10 is a package numerical choice, adjustable here. All negative eigenvalues, including roundoff, are recorded in dropped as their number, absolute total, and share of the absolute spectrum. Positive eigenvalues up to max_k define the FVE denominator. ncomponents is a positive integer or FVE; the default threshold 0.99 is fdapace's. The default max_k=max(1, min(length(fd.grid)-2, nobs(fd)-2)) follows fdapace 0.6.0's dense cross-sectional default (SetOptions.R), without the cap of 20 used on its smoothing route. An explicit dense max_k cannot exceed min(length(fd.grid), nobs(fd)-1). A fixed K exceeding max_k or the retained positive rank throws. A zero-variance sample also throws.

Signs make sum(phi .* mean) nonnegative, following fdapace. When its magnitude is at most sign_tol * norm(phi) * norm(mean), the largest absolute entry is made positive, with the first entry breaking ties. The relative tolerance 1e-12 is an adjustable package numerical choice; the fallback follows FunctionalGraphicalModels.jl. Orthonormality is under work-grid weights.

presmooth=nothing assumes negligible measurement noise, as in Ramsay and Silverman (2005), chapter 8. Noisy dense data should use presmooth or method=:pace. A LocalPolynomial or PenalizedSpline is applied through smooth_curves, evaluated on the original data grid, and recorded with its selected tuning metadata. Prediction applies the same fitted preprocessing; a spline GCV or REML choice is fixed at its training-selected lambda. All choices are stored and shown. training_data retains a copy of the original observations (before presmoothing) for fitted_curve_data. Inputs are copied. Dense noise variance and score covariances are nothing.

Derivatives

derivative(model) estimates velocity curves from a fitted FPCA. It returns an FPCADerivative with a mean derivative, derivative functions on the stored work grid, scores, bandwidths, kernel, and all choices. reconstruct(d) returns functional data; curve_data(d) and plot(d) draw the reconstructed derivative curves. mean_data(d) returns the mean derivative. These operations do not refit the model.

velocity_fit = derivative(model; mean_bandwidth=0.08,
    covariance_bandwidth=0.10)
velocity_curves = reconstruct(velocity_fit)
velocity_mean = mean_data(velocity_fit)
# With Plots loaded:
# plot(velocity_fit; curves=1:3)

The default method=:FPC differentiates the stored mean and each selected eigenfunction by local polynomial fitting, then combines them with the original scores. Orders one and two are supported. Derivative functions need not be orthogonal, and the retained eigenvalues describe the original curves. The default degree equals the derivative order, matching fdapace 0.6.0 FPCAder; this differs from the ν+1 default for standalone polynomial derivatives. fdapace labels second derivatives experimental. Accuracy still depends on the work-grid resolution, bandwidth, and smoothness of the fitted functions.

method=:DPC (Dai, Müller and Tao, 2018) instead estimates principal components of the derivative process, with its own eigenvalues and scores. It supports first derivatives and defaults to degree two and FVE(0.9999). Pass an integer or FVE via ncomponents to change that choice. It applies the polynomial derivative operator in both arguments of the covariance and reuses the usual weighted eigenstep. Dense fits use the sample covariance and project the correspondingly differentiated centered training curves. PACE and FACE use the full projected covariance and conditional-expectation scores from the original observations. The curve/derivative-score cross-covariance supplies the CE numerator. The parent fit's stored noise variance, including any estimated-noise floor, is reused without an additional ridge. Conditional score covariances omit uncertainty from fitting mean, covariance, and noise.

For FACE, DPC uses all retained components up to maxK in this denominator, including when the original fit selects fewer components. Masked training curves use their observed entries. This observation-space calculation forms grid-square derivative covariance matrices; it does not retain FACE's fitting and prediction costs on long grids.

DPC here uses stored quadrature weights, including nonuniform grids, and the same differentiated covariance for its derivative eigenvalues and score prior. fdapace's native DPC mixes finite differences, polynomial smoothing, rectangular quadrature, and an unsmoothed score-prior spectrum. Its DPC mean is also re-estimated from raw observations, whereas this package differentiates the fitted mean supplied by the user. The fdapace audit describes the resulting reference quantities and their validation. FPC1 is not implemented.

For either method the default kernel is Gaussian. With derivative order ν and domain width r, both default bandwidths are ν*0.10r when the fitted noise variance divided by the sum of selected original eigenvalues is at least 0.01, and ν*0.05r otherwise (including dense fits without a noise estimate). These rules follow fdapace 0.6.0 SetDerOptions. Supply a common bandwidth, or both mean_bandwidth and covariance_bandwidth. A partial pair or mixed forms throw. There is no bandwidth search. show reports resolved bandwidths, rule, degree, kernel, component selection and numerical tolerances.

For MultivariateFPCA, differentiation runs in node order and returns a MultivariateFPCADerivative. Shared or per-node keyword settings work as for marginal FPCA; component(d,j) retrieves the node result. Reconstruction, plotting, and mean data work nodewise, with shared or per-node grids. Both marginal and pooled fits support the two methods. Pooled FPC differentiates the shared basis and retains node scores. Pooled DPC uses each node's sample covariance and copied original training observations; its derivative components are node-specific.

Fixed-seed Fourier checks use simulate_kl with dense or sparse sampling. They check relative weighted derivative-function error and relative derivative-curve error below 0.12. Sparse checks supply the known mean, covariance and noise to isolate differentiation and CE reconstruction from covariance-smoothing bias. These are finite simulation checks, not general recovery guarantees.

Basis data use fit(FPCA, fd::BasisFunctionalData; lambda=0, penalty=nothing). This is the roughness-penalized method of Silverman (1996), implemented in fda 6.3.0 pca.fd. Eigenfunctions remain exact linear combinations of the input basis. Prediction requires that same basis. height_basis = to_basis(height, b)

(size(scores(smoothed)), eigvals(smoothed), smoothed.settings.lambda)

lambda=LambdaCV([0.0, 0.1, 1.0]) opts into leave-one-curve-out selection. For example, fit(FPCA, height_basis; lambda=LambdaCV([0.0, 0.1, 1.0]), ncomponents=2) compares two leading subspaces. fda does not provide an automatic selector for this function, so the default remains zero. FPCA with LambdaCV requires a positive integer ncomponents; the default FVE(0.99) remains available with a fixed lambda.

Reference, scale, and defaults

The reference sources are fda 6.3.0 pca.fd.R and fdPar.R. For centered coefficient rows C, Gram matrix G, and roughness matrix P, they solve

\[G(C^\top C/n)G h_j = \nu_j (G+\lambda P)h_j, \qquad h_j^\top(G+\lambda P)h_k=\delta_{jk}.\]

The source's inprod.R uses Romberg integration with a hard-coded relative stopping rule EPS=1e-4. Its B-spline fast path checks is.integer on the default Lfd objects and does not trigger for these calls. Even Fourier cross-products and harmonic-acceleration penalties can therefore have errors larger than 1e-8. The package uses its exact basis Gram/penalty matrices. The R generator keeps the complete pca.fd body and supplies exact matching-basis inner products in a private function environment. For the Fourier differential operator it supplies independently integrated products with rel.tol=1e-12, abs.tol=1e-14. No installed R namespace is modified. These are the documented exact-inner-product reference quantities compared at 1e-8; the CSVs also retain native pca.fd eigenvalues as native_eigenvalues. Native cca.fd uses eval.penalty for its Gram matrices and needs no such change for the second-derivative comparisons.

The denominator is n, including when centering is disabled. This differs from dense-grid FPCA's n-1. We keep R's eigenvalues ν and their variance proportions. Write qj² = hjᵀ G hj. The package harmonic is hj/qj, and its score is R's score divided by qj. Consequently the score second moment is νj/qj², rather than νj, for a positive penalty. With centering on, this is the score variance using denominator n. Harmonics have unit exact L² norm; positive-lambda harmonics generally are not L²-orthogonal. Reconstruction uses these L²-normalized harmonics and their inner-product scores, as for the other FPCA routes. It is not an orthogonal subspace projection at positive lambda. `settings.originalharmonicnorms` records all qj.

The grid-based derivative(f::FPCA) fit is unsupported for this route; DerivativeBasis(eigenfunctions(f), order) instead gives the exact derivative basis, and evaluate(f.mean, points; derivative=order) differentiates the mean.

LambdaCV follows Ramsay and Silverman (2005), §9.3.3, equation (9.3). It centers once using the overall mean, then fits each leave-one-curve-out sample without further centering. For each held-out curve it computes exact L² orthogonal projection residuals for subspaces m=1,…,K and sums their squared norms. Projection solves the subspace Gram system, since smoothed harmonics need not be L²-orthogonal. A positive integer ncomponents=K is required so that every fold and candidate uses the same number of subspaces. An FVE rule would compare sums with different numbers of terms and is rejected. The smallest total wins. Candidates remain in user order, and ties select the first. All scores, the criterion, centering rule, fold count, and selected index are in settings.lambda_selection. An invalid candidate/fold throws with its context rather than being silently dropped.

Reference values

Run Rscript test/reference/fda_m6.R with fda 6.3.0 to recreate the small CSV fixtures. Headers record the R and fda versions. The inputs are five-basis coefficient matrices for the 54 girls in growth (height and interval growth rate) and the 35 Canadian weather stations (temperature and log10 precipitation). Both datasets use cubic B-splines and Fourier bases. Lambdas are (0, 0.1, 10) for growth and (0, 1e4, 1e6) for weather. They are fixed values, with no optimizer comparison. Julia tests compare eigenvalues, harmonics on 101 points, scores, variance proportions, canonical correlations, weight functions, and variates at relative tolerance 1e-8.

For a nonzero lower domain endpoint, R's Fourier basis uses sin(ωt), while the package uses sin(ω(t-a)). The generator rotates coefficient pairs by ωa before exporting inputs. This orthogonal transformation preserves norms and penalties. FPCA comparisons apply the q_j normalization and package sign rule. CCA comparisons keep R's coefficient norm and apply paired signs in the rotated basis. The generator also evaluates both CV criteria independently through R fits. CI consumes these fixtures without running R.

Roughness-penalized basis FPCA, following fda 6.3.0 pca.fd/fdPar and Silverman (1996). Solve G*C*G*h = ν*(G + lambda*P)*h, where C is the centered coefficient cross-product divided by n (also when center=false). The default lambda is zero. The default penalty is derivative order max(0, order-2) for B-splines, harmonic acceleration for Fourier bases, and order zero for monomials, as in fdPar. Supply a derivative order, the Fourier symbol :harmonic_acceleration, or a symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix. LambdaCV(lambdas) opts into leave-one-curve-out selection and requires a positive integer ncomponents shared by all folds and candidates.

Eigenvalues and variance proportions keep pca.fd's penalized-metric scale. Each harmonic is divided by its exact L² norm q; scores are R's scores divided by q. Thus score variances (denominator n) are ν/q², and positive-lambda harmonics are individually L²-normalized but generally not L²-orthogonal. The package's FVE(0.99) default departs from R's nharm=2; a fixed integer reproduces nharm. The default max_k=min(nbasis,n-1) (n when uncentered) retains the available rank, unlike the grid routes' caps.

The same FPCA result supports exact basis prediction and reconstruction, and the existing plot-data layer and recipes. grid is only a display grid, defaulting to 201 equally spaced points as in curve_data; it never defines inner products. Mean and eigenfunctions retain exact basis coefficients. No noise model is assumed; noise and score covariances are nothing. All settings and input copies are retained.
