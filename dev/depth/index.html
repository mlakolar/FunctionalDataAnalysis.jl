Depth and outliers

A functional depth orders curves from the centre of a sample outwards. Deep curves are typical; curves of low depth are candidates for outliers. depth(fd, method) computes the depth of every curve with respect to its own sample, and depth(new, reference, method) the depth of new curves with respect to a reference sample. Both return a FunctionalDepth holding the depths, the ordering from deepest to least deep, the deepest curve (the functional median) and every setting used.

using FunctionalDataAnalysis
grid = collect(range(0, 1; length=31))
values = [a + b * sin(2pi * t) for (a, b) in zip(sin.(1:20), cos.(1:20) ./ 2), t in grid]
values[20, :] .+= 3                       # one shifted curve
fd = GridFunctionalData(values, grid)
d = depth(fd, ModifiedBandDepth())
(d.median, d.ordering[end], round.(d.values[[d.median, 20]]; digits=3))
(19, 20, [0.555, 0.1])
new = GridFunctionalData(values[1:3, :] .+ 0.1, grid)
depth(new, fd, FraimanMuniz()).values
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.3200000000000002
 0.27333333333333354
 0.7933333333333333

The modified epigraph and hypograph indices measure how much of the time a curve lies below or above the others; they underlie the outliergram.

(modified_epigraph_index(fd)[20], modified_hypograph_index(fd)[20])
(0.04999999999999998, 1.0000000000000002)

integration=:quadrature (the default for FraimanMuniz and ModifiedBandDepth, and for the indices) averages over the grid with the stored trapezoidal weights, which is the integral in the definitions and what fda.usc depth.MB does. integration=:uniform gives each grid point equal weight, as roahd and fda.usc depth.FM do. On uniform grids the two differ only through the half weights at the end points; on nonuniform grids equal weights over-count densely sampled stretches.

Modified band depth uses the rank formula: with a reference values strictly above x(t) and b strictly below, C(n, 2) - C(a, 2) - C(b, 2) pairs contain x(t). Sorting each grid column once costs O((n + n_ref) log n_ref · m) instead of the O(n · n_ref² · m) pairwise count; the tests check both agree, with ties. Band depth checks every pair, using bit masks of the grid points where a reference curve lies above or below x.

For a sample, the curve itself belongs to the reference: its pairs with every other curve contain it, and it counts in the empirical distributions. Except for roahd's compatibility options (BandDepth(definition=:roahd) and ModifiedBandDepth(ties=:average)), a sample's depth therefore equals its depth with respect to an identical reference (for random projections, with the same projections).

Curve representations

Depths compare curves on a common grid.

  • Complete GridFunctionalData uses its own grid. Target and reference must share it; use to_grid to change grids explicitly.
  • BasisFunctionalData is evaluated exactly on the grid keyword, which is required unless the other side is grid data.
  • IrregularFunctionalData, and grid data with missing values, are represented by FPCA reconstructions. FPCA is fitted to the reference with fit(FPCA, reference; method=:pace, fpca...) (PACE by default; fpca passes other FPCA keywords) and new curves are reconstructed from project_scores. When either side needs FPCA, both sides use the reconstruction. An existing FPCA fit can be passed directly.

representation (:grid, :basis_evaluated or :fpca_reconstruction) and the fitted FPCA are stored on the result, with one entry per component for multivariate data. The modified epigraph and hypograph indices accept complete grid data, basis data on a grid and FPCA fits; they do not fit FPCA themselves.

Multivariate data

For MultivariateFunctionalData, BandDepth and ModifiedBandDepth average the component depths with weights (uniform by default; nonnegative and summing to one), as roahd's multiBD and multiMBD do; components may have different grids. FraimanMuniz uses the Mahalanobis depth 1/(1 + d²) of the component vector at each grid point (fda.usc depth.FMp) and RandomProjectionDepth averages the Tukey depths of every component's projection on shared directions (fda.usc depth.RPp); both need components on one grid. ModalDepth uses the distance sqrt(Σ_c ‖x_c - y_c‖²) (fda.usc depth.modep).

Outlier displays

Three displays flag outlying curves and return plain numbers for plotting. Each result has a data function in the plot-data layer and a RecipesBase recipe: plot(result) or outlier_plot with Plots.jl loaded.

The functional boxplot (Sun and Genton, 2011) orders curves by a depth (any method of depth), takes the band of the deepest half as the central region and inflates it by 1.5 times its pointwise range for the fences. factor=AdjustedFactor() replaces 1.5 by the adjusted factor of Sun and Genton (2012), calibrated by simulating Gaussian curves with a robust covariance estimate so that about 0.7% of typical curves are flagged. Multivariate data are ordered by the multivariate depth and flagged when they leave the fences of any component.

shape = copy(values)
shape[7, :] .= 1.2 .* cos.(4pi .* grid)        # a curve of unusual shape
o = outliergram(GridFunctionalData(shape, grid))
(o.outliers, round(o.threshold; digits=4))
([7], 0.0388)

The outliergram (Arribas-Gil and Romo, 2014) plots MEI against MBD. A curve that crosses no other curve lies on the parabola a0 + a1·MEI + n²a0·MEI²; the distance below it grows with the number of crossings, and distances beyond Q3 + 1.5 IQR flag shape outliers. Curves at the top or bottom of the sample are checked again after a vertical shift, since a shape outlier that lies entirely above or below the others crosses none of them.

s = magnitude_shape(GridFunctionalData(shape, grid))
(s.outliers, argmax(s.variation_outlyingness), round(s.cutoff; digits=1))
([7, 20], 7, 2210.8)

The magnitude–shape plot (Dai and Genton, 2018) summarizes each curve by the mean MO and variation VO of its directional outlyingness: MO measures how far the curve sits from the pointwise median, VO how much its outlyingness changes along the curve. Squared robust Mahalanobis distances of (MO, VO) from the minimum covariance determinant estimate are compared with the Hardin–Rocke cutoff; above, the shifted curve 20 (large MO) and the shape outlier 7 (largest VO) are flagged. For multivariate data the outlyingness is the Stahel–Donoho outlyingness over random projection directions, and ‖MO‖ is plotted. The Hardin–Rocke cutoff is conservative in small samples, where its F approximation has few denominator degrees of freedom.

Worked example: Canadian temperatures

The full Canadian weather data (35 stations, daily temperatures; downloaded on first use, see the Datasets page) show the three displays side by side.

weather = load_dataset(:canadian_weather).data
temperature = component(weather, 1)
stations = ids(temperature)
stations[functional_boxplot(temperature).outliers]
# Scheffervll, Churchill, Yellowknife, Iqaluit, Inuvik, Resolute
stations[outliergram(temperature).outliers]
# St. Johns, Pr. Rupert
stations[magnitude_shape(temperature).outliers]
# Iqaluit, Inuvik, Resolute
using Random
stations[magnitude_shape(weather; rng=Xoshiro(1)).outliers]   # temperature and precipitation
# St. Johns, Sydney, Pr. Rupert, Resolute

The boxplot flags the cold northern stations, whose winters leave the fences. The outliergram flags the two maritime stations, St. John's and Prince Rupert, whose seasonal cycle is much flatter than the rest: their curves cross many others. The magnitude–shape plot flags the three Arctic stations with the most extreme magnitude; Prince Rupert has the next largest distance. With precipitation as a second component, the wet maritime stations and Resolute stand out.

Reference audit

The implementations follow the sources of roahd 1.4.3 and fda.usc 2.2.0 (current CRAN), read in full for the functions named here.

  • roahd BD and BD_relative count reference curves strictly above and strictly below x on the whole grid and multiply the counts. Pairs whose two curves cross while still enclosing x are missed, so the result can be smaller than the band depth of López-Pintado and Romo (2009); a curve at zero between (1, -1) and (-1, 1) has depth 1 by the definition and 2/3 in roahd. The default definition=:graph follows the definition; the test fixture includes data where the two differ.
  • roahd MBD (default manage_ties=FALSE) and MBD_relative use average ranks, which do not count bands exactly when values tie. manage_ties=TRUE and fda.usc depth.MB count exactly; this is the default here.
  • fda.usc depth.MB is internal (not exported) in 2.2.0. It weights grid points by the trapezoidal rule, while roahd's MBD and fda.usc's depth.FM give them equal weight.
  • fda.usc TD1 evaluates 1 - F(v-) as 1 - F(v - 1e-15). Once |v| > 16 the offset vanishes in double precision and a sample curve's projection no longer counts itself on the upper side: among projections (1, 2, 20) the largest gets Tukey depth 0 rather than 1/3. This package counts values >= v exactly; the golden fixture keeps projections far below 16 in magnitude, where the two agree.
  • fda.usc depth.RP projects with the trapezoidal inner product (inprod.fdata), depth.RPp with a plain sum over grid points. This package uses the trapezoidal inner product for both. The fixture projections vanish at both ends of a uniform grid, where the two inner products differ by the grid step, a positive factor Tukey depth ignores.
  • fda.usc depth.mode takes the bandwidth quantile over all reference distances, diagonal zeros included. The default quantile is then zero for samples of at most five curves (depth.mode, quantile 0.15) or nine curves (depth.modep, quantile 0.10), and R returns depth zero for every curve. This package throws and asks for a bandwidth. Both default quantiles are kept.
  • fda.usc mdepth.MhD (used by depth.FMp) replaces a singular covariance by an SVD pseudo-inverse with a warning; this package throws.
  • roahd's multiMBD requires sum(weights) == 1 exactly; this package accepts sums within sqrt(eps()) of one and does not renormalize.

The outlier displays follow roahd 1.4.3, fda 6.3.0, fdaoutlier 0.2.1 (current CRAN), MASS 7.3-65 and robustbase 0.99-7, read in full for the functions named in test/reference/outliers_m8.R, including fdaoutlier's C++ and MASS's lqs.c.

  • Central region. roahd fbplot takes every curve whose depth is at least the median depth, which with ties can be more than half the curves; fda fbplot takes exactly ⌈n/2⌉ curves in depth order, as Sun and Genton define it. central_ties=:first (default) follows the definition, central_ties=:all roahd.
  • Fences and median. roahd flags curves strictly outside a fence; fda also flags curves touching it, which matters only on exact equality. fda's median curve averages tied deepest curves; roahd and this package take the first. Both draw whiskers as the envelope of the curves not flagged.
  • Depth. roahd's fbplot uses MBD(manage_ties=TRUE), but inside its adjusted-factor search the simulated samples use MBD with average ranks; fda uses average ranks. Both weight grid points equally. This package uses one method throughout, by default the exact-tie trapezoidal MBD.
  • Adjusted factor. roahd estimates the covariance with robustbase covOGK(sigmamu=s_Qn), whose pairwise covariances use the τ-scale (the Qn scale is not passed on to covGK), and returns the raw, not the reweighted, OGK matrix. Each trial's factor comes from uniroot on [0.5, 5] with tolerance 1e-3, although the flagged proportion is a step function of the factor; this package computes the factor exactly from the simulated curves' thresholds and needs no bracket. roahd does not adjust multivariate boxplots.
  • robustbase Qn truncates candidate differences to single precision during its search ((float)(y[i] - y[n - jh]) in qn_sn.c) and returns the truncated value when the search ends early, so it can differ from the exact order statistic by about 1e-8 relative (in the fixture, 1.4236938223 against 1.4236938612). This package computes the exact order statistic with robustbase's constants.
  • Outliergram. roahd's outliergram computes MBD with average ranks and equal weights, flags distances >= Q3 + 1.5 IQR (quantile type 7), and shifts candidates by the smallest gap to the second-lowest (or second-highest) curve where they are extreme. Two defects: when the first stage flags no curve, MEI_data[-ID_shape_outlier] is empty and the shift check is skipped; and candidates are matched with grep(ID, ID_to_check), a regular-expression match that confuses, for example, curves 1 and 16. This package always runs the check (shift=false reproduces roahd in the first case) and matches indices exactly.
  • Directional outlyingness. fdaoutlier's univariate dir_out uses R's median and mad, the mean over grid points and, for VO, the sample variance (divisor m - 1); the multivariate version divides by m. Its multivariate projection depth (C++ projectionDepth) takes the one-sided maximum of (u'x - med)/MAD over directions, with the upper median and an unscaled MAD, instead of the absolute value in Zuo's projection depth; its directions are uniform in the cube [-1, 1]^d and normalized, regenerated from the same seed at every grid point. definition=:fdaoutlier reproduces all of this; the default uses the two-sided outlyingness, quadrature weights and the population variance.
  • MCD. MASS cov.rob(method="mcd") divides each column by its IQR, enumerates all (p + 1)-subsets when there are fewer than 5000 (otherwise samples min(500(p + 1), 3000)), runs up to four concentration steps per subset, stopping when the log determinant falls by less than 0.1%, and reweights with a (1 + 15/(n - p))² inflation and a 97.5% χ² cut. This package ports that search exactly. fdaoutlier measures distances from the reweighted estimate; Dai and Genton apply the Hardin–Rocke cutoff to the raw MCD estimate (the subset's mean and covariance), which is the default here.
  • Hardin–Rocke cutoff. fdaoutlier's hardin_factor_numeric reads a table of simulated factors for n < 1000 and q ≤ 3, and otherwise computes Croux–Haesbroeck factors with c estimated by Monte Carlo from rchisq(10000, q, h/n), which passes h/n as the noncentrality rather than using the h/n quantile; the resulting c is about twice Hardin and Rocke's P(χ²_{q+2} < χ²_{q,h/n})/(h/n). Its cutoff is therefore random and differs from the formula. This package evaluates the formula exactly (its F quantile matches fdaoutlier's deterministic part) and accepts a numeric cutoff for fdaoutlier's value.

Validation

test/reference/depth_m8.R generates small fixtures (12 curves on 15 grid points, a tied variant rounded to one decimal with a duplicated curve, a nonuniform grid and a two-component multivariate sample) and writes test/data/m8_depth_input.csv and test/data/m8_depth_golden.csv with R 4.5.1, roahd 1.4.3 and fda.usc 2.2.0. Recreate them from the repository root with Rscript test/reference/depth_m8.R. The tests compare roahd BD, BD_relative, MBD (both tie options), MBD_relative, MEI, MHI, multiMBD, multiBD and fda.usc depth.FM, depth.MB, depth.RP, depth.mode (with its bandwidth), depth.FMp, depth.RPp and depth.modep, for samples and for new curves, at relative error below 1e-8, using the options above that reproduce each reference. The band depth by definition is compared with a direct pairwise count in the same script.

test/reference/outliers_m8.R writes test/data/m8_outliers_input.csv and test/data/m8_outliers_golden.csv with R 4.5.1, roahd 1.4.3, fda 6.3.0, fdaoutlier 0.2.1, MASS 7.3-65 and robustbase 0.99-7; recreate them with Rscript test/reference/outliers_m8.R. The fixtures are 24 curves on 20 points with magnitude and shape outliers, a variant with tied depths at the median, and a two-component sample of 20 curves. The tests compare, at relative error below 1e-8 and with exact flagged indices:

  • roahd fbplot depths, central envelopes, fences and outliers for univariate, tied and multivariate data (method=ModifiedBandDepth(integration=:uniform), central_ties=:all), and fda fbplot depths, outliers and median (method=ModifiedBandDepth(ties=:average, integration=:uniform));
  • roahd outliergram MEI, MBD, distances, quartiles, first-stage and final outliers (method=ModifiedBandDepth(ties=:average, integration=:uniform)), on data where the shift check adds a curve;
  • fdaoutlier msplot and dir_out MO, VO, distances, MCD subset, center, covariance, outliers and median for univariate and bivariate data (definition=:fdaoutlier, fdaoutlier's directions and tabulated cutoff), and the raw MCD estimate and rescaled VO of the default definition;
  • the F quantile of fdaoutlier's croux_hesbroeck_asymptotic and the exact Hardin–Rocke c;
  • robustbase covOGK with the exact Qn (and Qn itself where it is exact), and one adjusted-factor trial on a stored simulated sample: roahd's uniroot root lies within its reported precision of the exact factor, and both flag the same curves.
FunctionalDataAnalysis.depth — Function
depth(fd, method; weights=nothing, grid=nothing, fpca=(;))
depth(fd, reference, method; weights=nothing, grid=nothing, fpca=(;))

Functional depth of the curves in fd with respect to their own sample, or of new curves fd with respect to the curves in reference. method is one of FraimanMuniz, BandDepth, ModifiedBandDepth, RandomProjectionDepth and ModalDepth. Returns a FunctionalDepth with the depths, the ordering, the deepest curve and the settings used. For every method except roahd's compatibility options (BandDepth(definition=:roahd), ModifiedBandDepth(ties=:average)), the depth of a sample equals its depth with respect to an identical reference (for random projections, with the same projections).

Depths compare curves on a common grid:

  • complete GridFunctionalData uses its grid; target and reference must share it;
  • BasisFunctionalData is evaluated exactly on grid, which is then required (or taken from grid data on the other side);
  • IrregularFunctionalData, or grid data with missing values, is represented by its FPCA reconstruction on the work grid (or grid). FPCA is fitted to the reference curves with fit(FPCA, reference; method=:pace, fpca...), so PACE is the default and fpca passes any other FPCA keywords; target curves are reconstructed from project_scores. When either side needs FPCA, both sides use the reconstruction. An existing FPCA fit can be passed as fd or as reference instead.

The representation and the FPCA fit are recorded on the result.

For MultivariateFunctionalData each component is brought to a grid in the same way (grid may be one grid or one per component). weights (one nonnegative weight per component, summing to one; uniform by default as in roahd) apply to BandDepth and ModifiedBandDepth, which average component depths; the other methods follow fda.usc's multivariate versions described in their docstrings.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.FunctionalDepth — Type
FunctionalDepth

Result of depth. values holds one depth per target curve and ordering the curve indices from deepest to least deep (ties keep curve order). median is the index of the deepest curve (the first one on ties, as R's which.max) and median_curve its values on the evaluation grid, as GridFunctionalData or, for multivariate data, MultivariateFunctionalData. method is the depth method; reference is :sample for the depth of a sample with respect to itself and :reference for new curves, with nreference reference curves. representation records how curves reached a common grid (:grid, :basis_evaluated or :fpca_reconstruction; one entry per component for multivariate data) and fpca the FPCA fits used (nothing where none was needed). settings stores every resolved choice: integration weights, ties, bandwidth, projections, component weights and the evaluation grids. ids are the target subject labels.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.FraimanMuniz — Type
FraimanMuniz(; scale=nothing, integration=:quadrature)

Fraiman–Muniz integrated depth (Fraiman and Muniz, 2001): the average over the grid of the univariate depth 1 - |1/2 - F(x(t))|, where F is the empirical distribution of the reference curves at t (counting values <= x(t)).

scale=true (the univariate default, from fda.usc 2.2.0 depth.FM, which passes scale=TRUE to FM1) uses 1 - 2|1/2 - F|, with range [0, 1]; scale=false is the original [1/2, 1] form. For multivariate data the pointwise depth is the Mahalanobis depth 1/(1 + d²) of the component vector at each grid point, with the reference mean and covariance (denominator n-1), as in fda.usc depth.FMp with its default mdepth.MhD; scale must then be left at nothing. A singular reference covariance throws (fda.usc falls back to an SVD pseudo-inverse with a warning).

integration=:quadrature (default) averages with the stored trapezoidal weights, the integral in the definition; :uniform gives every grid point equal weight, as fda.usc does. The two agree up to the end-point weights on uniform grids. The departure follows the package rule that integrals use quadrature weights on possibly nonuniform grids.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.BandDepth — Type
BandDepth(; definition=:graph)

Band depth with bands formed by two curves (J = 2; López-Pintado and Romo, 2009): the proportion of pairs of reference curves whose band min(x_j, x_k) <= x <= max(x_j, x_k) contains the whole graph of x. For the depth of a sample with respect to itself the pairs include the curve itself, which always contain it.

definition=:graph (default) is that definition on the grid, counted with bit masks in O(n · n_ref² · m / 64) operations. definition=:roahd reproduces roahd 1.4.3 BD and BD_relative, which count only pairs with one curve strictly above and the other strictly below on the whole grid ((N_above · N_below + n - 1) / C(n, 2) for a sample). That count misses pairs whose curves cross while still enclosing x, so it can be smaller; the departure follows the published definition. For multivariate data the depth is the weighted average of the component depths (roahd multiBD), with uniform weights by default.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.ModifiedBandDepth — Type
ModifiedBandDepth(; ties=:exact, integration=:quadrature)

Modified band depth (López-Pintado and Romo, 2009): the average over the grid of the proportion of pairs of reference curves whose band contains x(t). For a sample the pairs include the curve itself. It is computed from ranks in O((n + n_ref) log n_ref · m) operations: with a reference values strictly above x(t) and b strictly below, C(n_ref, 2) - C(a, 2) - C(b, 2) pairs contain x(t).

ties=:exact (default) is that count, which is exact with ties; it equals fda.usc 2.2.0's depth.MB and roahd 1.4.3's MBD(manage_ties=TRUE). ties=:average reproduces roahd's default MBD(manage_ties=FALSE) and MBD_relative, which use average ranks; the two agree without ties. integration is as in FraimanMuniz: roahd weights grid points equally (:uniform), fda.usc depth.MB uses the normalized trapezoidal weights (:quadrature, the default). For multivariate data the depth is the weighted average of the component depths (roahd multiMBD), with uniform weights by default.

source
FunctionalDataAnalysis.RandomProjectionDepth — Type
RandomProjectionDepth(; nproj=nothing, projections=nothing, scale=nothing, rng=Random.default_rng())

Random-projection depth (Cuevas, Febrero and Fraiman, 2007; fda.usc 2.2.0 depth.RP): the average over projections z_j of the univariate Tukey depth min(F(v), 1 - F(v-)) of v = ⟨x, z_j⟩ among the reference projections. Inner products use the stored trapezoidal weights, as fda.usc's inprod.fdata.

By default nproj = 50 projections (fda.usc's default) are drawn from the zero-mean Gaussian process with covariance exp(-|s - t|/θ), θ = 0.2 × domain length, and normalized to unit L² norm (fda.usc rproc2fdata with sigma="vexponential"), using rng. Supplying projections (an nproj × m matrix of functions on the evaluation grid) uses them as given. The projections used are stored on the result.

scale=nothing resolves to false for univariate data (fda.usc depth.RP, depth in [0, 1/2]) and true for multivariate data (fda.usc depth.RPp, whose default mdepth.TD doubles the depth). For multivariate data, as in depth.RPp, every component is projected on the same z_j and the Tukey depths are averaged over components and projections; all components must share a grid. fda.usc's depth.RPp projects with a plain sum rather than the trapezoidal inner product; this package uses quadrature for both.

fda.usc evaluates F(v-) as F(v - 1e-15), which equals F(v) once |v| > 16 in double precision, so its depth of a sample curve with respect to its own sample then omits the curve itself from one side. This package counts reference values >= v exactly.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.ModalDepth — Type
ModalDepth(; bandwidth=nothing, quantile=nothing, kernel=GaussianKernel())

Modal (h-) depth (Cuevas, Febrero and Fraiman, 2007; fda.usc 2.2.0 depth.mode): D(x) = Σ_j K(‖x - x_j‖ / h) over the reference curves, with the L² distance under the stored trapezoidal weights (fda.usc metric.lp, trapezoidal rule). The sum is not normalized, as in fda.usc, so values grow with the reference size. The kernel is a KernelSmoothing.jl kernel; the default Gaussian kernel is fda.usc's Ker.norm.

The bandwidth is bandwidth when given; otherwise the quantile (R type 7) of all reference-to-reference distances, diagonal zeros included, as fda.usc computes it. quantile=nothing resolves to 0.15 for univariate data (depth.mode) and 0.10 for multivariate data (depth.modep). Multivariate distances are sqrt(Σ_c ‖x_c - y_c‖²) over components (fda.usc metric.ldata with method="euclidean"); components may have different grids. The resolved bandwidth is stored on the result. A zero quantile (at most five curves at 0.15, nine at 0.10, since the diagonal zeros count) throws; fda.usc then returns depth zero for every curve.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.modified_epigraph_index — Function
modified_epigraph_index(fd; integration=:quadrature, grid=nothing, weights=nothing)
modified_epigraph_index(fd, reference; integration=:quadrature, grid=nothing, weights=nothing)

Modified epigraph index (López-Pintado and Romo, 2011): the average over the grid of the proportion of reference curves lying on or above each curve, MEI(x) = Σ_j λ{t : x_j(t) >= x(t)} / (n λ(I)). For a sample the curve itself counts. Low values mark curves high in the sample. It equals roahd 1.4.3 MEI with integration=:uniform; the default uses the stored trapezoidal weights, as in ModifiedBandDepth. Multivariate data give the weighted average of component indices (roahd multiMEI; uniform weights by default).

Curves must be complete grid data, basis data on grid, or an FPCA fit (its reconstruction); irregular or incomplete curves throw, since an index needs an explicit common-grid representation. Returns a vector.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.modified_hypograph_index — Function
modified_hypograph_index(fd; integration=:quadrature, grid=nothing, weights=nothing)
modified_hypograph_index(fd, reference; integration=:quadrature, grid=nothing, weights=nothing)

Modified hypograph index (López-Pintado and Romo, 2009): the average over the grid of the proportion of reference curves lying on or below each curve, MHI(x) = Σ_j λ{t : x_j(t) <= x(t)} / (n λ(I)). FunctionalDataAnalysis.functional_boxplot — Function
functional_boxplot(fd; method=ModifiedBandDepth(), factor=1.5, central=0.5,
                   central_ties=:first, weights=nothing, grid=nothing, fpca=(;))

Functional boxplot (Sun and Genton, 2011). Curves are ordered by the depth method (computed by depth, with weights, grid and fpca as there); the central region is the band spanned by the deepest proportion central of the curves, and the fences inflate it by factor times its pointwise range on each side. Curves crossing a fence anywhere are flagged as magnitude outliers. Returns a FunctionalBoxplot.

Defaults: depth MBD (fda 6.3.0 fbplot and roahd 1.4.3 fbplot), here with the package's exact-tie, trapezoidal ModifiedBandDepth; factor 1.5 and central proportion 0.5 (both references). central_ties=:first takes exactly ⌈n · central⌉ curves, ties in depth broken by curve order, as the definition and fda's fbplot; :all takes every curve whose depth is at least the 1 - central quantile, as roahd (which then may include more curves). A curve is flagged when it lies strictly outside a fence, as roahd; fda also flags curves touching a fence, which matters only on exact equality. The median is the first deepest curve (roahd); fda averages tied deepest curves.

roahd is reproduced by method=ModifiedBandDepth(integration=:uniform) with central_ties=:all, fda by method=ModifiedBandDepth(ties=:average, integration=:uniform). factor=AdjustedFactor() selects the adjusted factor of Sun and Genton (2012); see AdjustedFactor.

Multivariate data use the multivariate depth (for MBD, roahd's multiMBD weighted average) to order curves, one envelope and pair of fences per component, and flag a curve outside the fences of any component, as roahd's fbplot for mfData.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.AdjustedFactor — Type
AdjustedFactor(; ntrials=20, trial_size=nothing, rate=2Φ(4Φ⁻¹(1/4)), rng=Random.default_rng())

The adjusted fence factor of Sun and Genton (2012) for functional_boxplot, following roahd 1.4.3 fbplot(adjust=list(...)). The curves' covariance is estimated robustly by the orthogonalized Gnanadesikan–Kettenring estimator (robustbase 0.99-7 covOGK(sigmamu=s_Qn), two iterations, Qn scales and τ-scale pairwise covariances, as roahd; the Qn scale is the exact order statistic, which robustbase can truncate to single precision). Each of ntrials trials draws trial_size Gaussian curves (default 8n, roahd's) with that covariance around the median curve, and finds the factor at which the boxplot of the simulated sample flags the proportion rate of its curves. The adjusted factor is the average over trials. The default rate, 2Φ(4Φ⁻¹(1/4)) ≈ 0.007, is the share of Gaussian observations outside the classical boxplot fences, as in roahd.

roahd finds each trial's factor with uniroot on [0.5, 5] with tolerance 1e-3. The flagged proportion is a step function of the factor: a simulated curve is flagged exactly when the factor is below its own threshold max_t max((x - U)/(U - L), (L - x)/(U - L)) (central envelope [L, U]). This package therefore takes each trial's factor exactly, as the (⌊rate · trial_size⌋ + 1)-th largest threshold, the smallest factor flagging at most the proportion rate; no bracket is needed. A rate at or above the share of curves outside the central region would give a negative factor (fences inside the central region); this throws an ArgumentError, where roahd's bracket would make uniroot fail. Simulations use rng; the trial factors are stored on the result. Univariate data only, as in roahd.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.outliergram — Function
outliergram(fd; method=ModifiedBandDepth(), factor=1.5, p_check=0.05, shift=true,
            grid=nothing, fpca=(;))

Outliergram (Arribas-Gil and Romo, 2014): the modified epigraph index against the modified band depth. For a curve that does not cross the others the point (MEI, MBD) lies on the parabola a0 + a1·MEI + a2·MEI² (a0 = -2/(n(n-1)), a1 = 2(n+1)/(n-1), a2 = n²a0); shape outliers lie far below it. Curves whose distance below the parabola is at least Q3 + factor · IQR of the distances (R quantile type 7) are flagged.

With shift=true, curves not flagged whose MEI is at most the p_check quantile (curves high in the sample) or at least the 1 - p_check quantile (low curves) are checked again after a vertical shift: a curve that is the pointwise maximum (minimum) somewhere is moved down (up) by the smallest gap to the second-largest (second-smallest) value at those points, its MEI and MBD recomputed within the sample, and it is flagged if its distance then reaches the threshold. Returns an Outliergram.

Defaults follow roahd 1.4.3 outliergram: factor=1.5, p_check=0.05, shift check on. method is the ModifiedBandDepth and its integration also weights the MEI; the package default is exact ties and trapezoidal weights. roahd's outliergram uses MBD with average ranks and equal weights, which method=ModifiedBandDepth(ties=:average, integration=:uniform) reproduces. Two departures from roahd, documented on the "Depth and outliers" page: roahd skips the shift check when the first stage flags no curve, and matches shift candidates with grep, which confuses indices such as 1 and 12; this package checks every candidate.

Univariate data only. Curves reach a common grid as in depth.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.magnitude_shape — Function
magnitude_shape(fd; definition=:dai_genton, integration=nothing, nproj=nothing,
                directions=nothing, rng=Random.default_rng(), cutoff=:hardin_rocke,
                nsamp=:best, grid=nothing, fpca=(;))

Magnitude–shape plot and its outlier rule (Dai and Genton, 2018), following fdaoutlier 0.2.1 msplot. The directional outlyingness of curve i at t is O_i(t) = SDO(x_i(t)) · v_i(t), with the Stahel–Donoho outlyingness SDO (the reciprocal projection depth minus one) of x_i(t) among the curves at t and v_i(t) the unit vector from the pointwise median to x_i(t). For univariate data O_i(t) = (x_i(t) - med(t)) / MAD(t) (R's mad, constant 1.4826). The mean MO_i = ∫ O_i and variation VO_i = ∫ ‖O_i - MO_i‖² (with ∫ the normalized grid average) are the magnitude and shape coordinates. The minimum covariance determinant (MCD) subset of (MO, VO) is found by the search of MASS 7.3-65 cov.rob(method="mcd", nsamp="best"), as fdaoutlier; curves whose squared robust Mahalanobis distance exceeds cutoff are flagged. Returns a MagnitudeShape.

  • integration=nothing resolves to :quadrature (stored trapezoidal weights, the package rule for integrals) for definition=:dai_genton and to :uniform (equal weights, fdaoutlier) for :fdaoutlier.
  • Multivariate data (p components on one grid) use SDO = max_u |u'x - med| / MAD over projection directions u, with the pointwise median the curve value of smallest outlyingness, as fdaoutlier. directions (nproj × p, rows normalized here) are used as given; otherwise nproj (default 200, as fdaoutlier) directions are drawn uniformly on the sphere with rng. fdaoutlier draws them uniformly in the cube [-1, 1]^p and normalizes, which favours the cube's diagonals.
  • definition=:dai_genton (default) measures distances from the raw MCD estimate, the mean and sample covariance of the subset, to which the Hardin–Rocke cutoff applies (Dai and Genton, 2018).
  • definition=:fdaoutlier reproduces fdaoutlier's computations exactly: distances from MASS's reweighted estimate (cov.rob's center and cov); for multivariate data its one-sided max_u (u'x - med) / MAD with upper medians and an unscaled MAD; for univariate data VO as the sample variance over grid points (divisor m - 1). Multivariate VO divides by m in both definitions.
  • cutoff=:hardin_rocke (default) is the Hardin and Rocke (2005) approximation c(m - q + 1)/(q m) d² ~ F(q, m - q + 1) at level 0.993 (Dai and Genton), q = p + 1, with m from the Croux–Haesbroeck asymptotics and Hardin and Rocke's small-sample correction, and the exact c = P(χ²_{q+2} < χ²_{q, h/n}) / (h/n), h = ⌊(n + q + 1)/2⌋. fdaoutlier instead uses a table of simulated factors for n < 1000 and q ≤ 3 and otherwise estimates c by Monte Carlo from noncentral χ² draws (passing h/n as the noncentrality), about twice the exact value; its cutoff is therefore random and not reproduced. A positive number gives the cutoff for squared distances directly (for example fdaoutlier's).
  • nsamp follows MASS: :best enumerates all q + 1-subsets when there are fewer than 5000 and otherwise samples min(500(q + 1), 3000) of them with rng; :exact or an integer may be given. The settings record which.

Curves reach a common grid as in depth.
