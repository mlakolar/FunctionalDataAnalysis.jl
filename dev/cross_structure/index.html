Cross-structure

These tools summarize dependence between two processes measured on the same subjects. They complement a conditional-dependence graph: cross-covariance, concurrent correlation and dynamic correlation are marginal summaries. Subject counts must agree, and available subject IDs must match in order.

Functional singular components

using FunctionalDataAnalysis
import StatsAPI

weather = load_dataset(:canadian_weather; sample=true).data
temperature = component(weather, 1)
precipitation = component(weather, 2)
f = StatsAPI.fit(FSVD, temperature, precipitation; ncomponents=1)
svdvals(f)
left_functions(f), right_functions(f)
left_scores(f), right_scores(f)
fve(f)[1:ncomponents(f)]

FSVD decomposes the cross-covariance operator. The two processes may have unequal domains and nonuniform grids. With stored diagonal quadrature matrices W1,W2, the package computes svd(sqrt(W1)*C*sqrt(W2)), then divides the left and right vectors by their respective square-root weights. Each set of functions is orthonormal under its stored quadrature. The largest absolute entry of each left function is positive; the paired right function and scores use the same sign. Repeated singular values identify subspaces rather than unique functions.

Complete grid pairs use sample means and the fdapace FSVD divisor n−2. This differs from standalone CrossCovariance, whose divisor is n−1. The default component cap is min(20,n-2) and default selection is FVE(0.9999), applied to cumulative squared singular values within the cap. A fixed integer disables FVE selection and throws if the retained rank is too small. Values at most 1e-10 of the largest are discarded. At least three subjects are required. fve(f) retains the full cumulative curve within the cap; f.selection records the chosen count and criterion. nobs(f) counts paired training subjects. Singular-value, function, score and FVE accessors return stored arrays; use copy before changing them.

If either node is irregular or masked, both marginal fits use PACE, including a complete-grid partner, and the cross surface uses SmoothedCrossCovariance. This provides marginal measurement-error variances for conditional scores. method=:auto is resolved to :pace; explicit method=:dense is rejected. settings.fpca_options1/2 retain the requested options and settings.effective_fpca_options1/2 record the options passed to FPCA. Supply bandwidth as a number, a pair of axis bandwidths, or explicit-candidate CurveCV. The Gaussian cross-smoothing kernel is the default. Equal-time pairs are retained, assuming independent measurement errors between nodes. The marginal means, covariances and noise variances come from the existing FPCA interface; pass named tuples through fpca_options1 and fpca_options2 to change their tuning or supply inputs. Dense presmoothing must be performed explicitly on the input data.

Dense singular scores are quadrature integrals of centered curves. Sparse scores condition jointly on both observed processes. The stacked marginal and cross-covariance surface is projected to be positive semidefinite using the existing eigen step. Marginal noise variances are then added before linear interpolation to observation times. The conditional solver requires a positive definite observation covariance and a full-row-rank interpolation matrix. Three observations in one linear-interpolation cell have only two grid coordinates and lose row rank. Adding noise on the grid cannot repair that design: H * A * H' is singular whenever H lacks full row rank, even when the grid covariance A is positive definite. Refine the work grids to separate observation times, or change the observation design. On factorization failure, the error reports the numerical interpolation row rank; if it is full, positive marginal noise variances can address a singular signal covariance. Rank is computed only on failure. No extra regularization or rho is introduced. All fits, tuning, component selection and numerical conventions are retained on the result.

Concurrent correlation

cc = concurrent_correlation(temperature, precipitation; bandwidth=30.0)
cc.grid, cc.correlation
cc.affected1, cc.affected2

This estimates corr(X(t),Y(t)) for simultaneous observations. Observation times must agree within every subject. The estimator assumes no measurement error and uses five local linear smooths: two means at pooled observed times, then two squared residuals and one residual cross-product at the output grid. All five smooths use KernelSmoothing with observation weights one.

Bandwidth is required, as in fdapace FCCor. Supply one positive number or one/five numbers in a vector or tuple. Five numbers are ordered as mean X, mean Y, variance X, variance Y, cross-product. The actual source default kernel is Epanechnikov. Output defaults to sorted unique observation times; grid may select another increasing grid inside that range. A nonpositive variance estimate is replaced by NaN with a warning. affected1 and affected2 retain the affected masks. Correlations are not clipped to [−1,1]. The result also stores all five bandwidths, kernel, means, variances, covariance and their grids.

Dynamic correlation

dc = dynamic_correlation(temperature, precipitation)
dc.correlation             # average over stations
dc.individual              # one value per station

dynamic_correlation requires complete curves on the same grid with matching weights. For each process it subtracts each curve's time average, subtracts the population mean of the resulting curves, and scales each residual curve to time RMS one. Each individual correlation is the time-averaged inner product of the paired standardized curves. The reported correlation averages those values over subjects. It describes synchrony of residual trajectories, not pointwise association or conditional independence.

Stored quadrature weights are divided by the domain length for every time average. There are no bandwidth or component defaults. Missing values require an explicit conversion before fitting; they are not silently interpolated as in R. Zero residual RMS is undefined and throws. The result stores individual correlations, standardized curves, grid, weights and centering conventions.

Reference validation

test/reference/fdapace_m6.R uses fdapace 0.6.0. Run it from the repository root with Rscript test/reference/fdapace_m6.R; CI reads its committed CSVs without running R. Headers record the generating R and package versions. Tests require relative errors below 1e-8 for singular values, both functions, both score matrices, cumulative fractions, the concurrent curve and individual dynamic correlations. Largest-entry-positive left-function signs are used in both languages.

The dense fixture has singular functions that vanish at the endpoints, so fdapace's rectangular operator and the weighted operator agree. Its values, functions and scores are compared directly with native FSVD. Sparse inputs reuse the milestone 4 cross-covariance fixture with fixed bandwidths and supplied marginal means, covariances and noise. The native sparse FSVD results are preserved as reference-only m6_sparse_native_*.csv, which Julia tests do not read. Since the discretizations differ, the compared sparse quantity is an independent R weighted SVD of the native cross surface, followed by joint conditional scores with weighted marginal integrals. The R script spells out that calculation. It retains the reference's unweighted PSD projection of the stacked surface. FCCor and DynCorr are compared directly with native outputs. No optimizer agreement or benchmark claim is inferred from these fixed-tuning comparisons.

The fdapace source audit details defaults, source discrepancies and deliberate departures.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.FSVD — Type
fit(FSVD, fd1, fd2; ncomponents=FVE(0.9999), max_k=nothing, maxK=nothing,
    bandwidth=nothing, kernel=KernelSmoothing.GaussianKernel(),
    fpca_options1=(;), fpca_options2=(;))

Functional singular component analysis of paired grid or irregular curves (Yang, Müller and Stadtmüller, 2011). Subject counts and available IDs must agree in order. Both marginal FPCA fits, cross-covariance, singular values, functions, scores, cumulative squared cross-covariance fractions, and settings are stored. Inputs are copied.

Defaults follow fdapace 0.6.0 FSVD / SetSVDOptions: FVE 0.9999, max_k=min(20,n-2), Gaussian smoothing, scores enabled, and independent cross-node measurement errors (equal-time products retained). Fixed component counts disable FVE selection. At least three subjects are required. Marginal FPCA options are named tuples passed to FPCA; empty tuples use its source-audited defaults, including 51 work-grid points for PACE. Unless both nodes are complete grids, FSVD forces method=:pace for both marginals, including a complete-grid partner, so each has a measurement-error variance for conditional scores. Explicit method=:dense is rejected on this route. The requested and effective marginal options are recorded in settings.

Complete grid pairs reuse CrossCovariance, rescaled to the reference FSVD divisor n-2 (distinct from the standalone sample covariance's n-1). Otherwise SmoothedCrossCovariance uses the marginal means and work grids. bandwidth is required on this route: a scalar, pair, or explicit CurveCV. This inherits the existing whole-subject CV contract in place of fdapace's pair-level GCV. kernel applies to cross smoothing only; marginal kernels are controlled by their FPCA options.

As in package FPCA, the SVD uses stored quadrature weights on both axes, including nonuniform grids. This deliberately differs from fdapace's ordinary matrix SVD and first-spacing singular-value scaling. Functions are mutually orthonormal on each axis. Each left function's largest absolute entry is positive; the right function and both scores receive the same sign. Relative singular values at most 1e-10 of the largest are discarded before selection. Dense scores are quadrature integrals. Sparse scores condition jointly on both processes, using the existing PACE solver, PSD projection of the stacked marginal/cross covariance, and the marginal noise variances; no rho is added.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.concurrent_correlation — Function
concurrent_correlation(fd1, fd2; bandwidth,
    kernel=KernelSmoothing.EpanechnikovKernel(), grid=nothing)

Concurrent correlation of simultaneously observed paired processes, following fdapace 0.6.0 FCCor. Observation times must agree within each subject; unmatched observations throw. bandwidth is required: one positive number or five in mean1, mean2, variance1, variance2, cross-product order. The source's actual default kernel is Epanechnikov, despite its help text saying Gaussian. grid=nothing uses sorted unique observation times. Five local linear smooths use KernelSmoothing with observation weights one. Means are estimated at the pooled observed times before residual products are smoothed on the output grid. This assumes no measurement error. Nonpositive variance estimates yield NaN and a warning; affected points are recorded separately for each process. The copied result retains all grids, five bandwidths, kernel, degree, means, variances, covariance, correlation, and affected masks. No clipping is applied.

FunctionalDataAnalysis.dynamic_correlation — Function
dynamic_correlation(fd1::GridFunctionalData, fd2::GridFunctionalData)

Dynamical correlation of paired complete curves on the same grid, following fdapace 0.6.0 DynCorr (Dubin and Müller, 2005). First subtract each curve's time average, then the cross-sectional mean of those centered curves. Divide each residual curve by its time RMS, integrate the paired standardized curves, and average the individual correlations over subjects. All integrals use the stored quadrature weights divided by the domain length. Both individual correlations and their mean are returned, with copied standardized curves, weights and grid. The source has no tuning defaults. Missing data throw; unlike R, this routine does not silently interpolate them. A zero residual RMS throws an informative error instead of returning an undefined correlation.
